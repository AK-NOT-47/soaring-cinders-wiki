Golems

CANONCaelian OdysseyMixed
First conceived 2023-02-16 · last updated 2023-02-16

Golems CANON

Soul-forged stone servants — created by Gideon from harvested criminal souls, unnerving enough that even Locke Evertide is uneasy around them.

Summary — Golems are a baseline enemy type with a dark origin: Gideon Azurith gathers criminals and outlaws (via Citrine), harvests their souls, and converts them into golems — “literal conversion of people into tools” — which he justifies by sending the golems to help people (2023-02-16). Locke “is a little creeped out by the golems for some reason” (2023-02-16) — narrative unease tied to that soul-harvested origin. The playable proto’s only golem is the boss-tier tomb guardian (owned on the Ossuary Sentinel page).

Description & role

  • Listed among the baseline enemy types in the brainstorm test setup: Goblins, Slimes, Golems, Brigands (2023-02-16).
  • Heavy stone constructs; in the source’s screenplay vignette Locke’s borrowed sword “SHATTERS against its stone body,” and she must find weak points (joints) to bring one down — an early illustration of the “tactical, not brute-force” combat ethos (Enemy Design Philosophy).

Origin CANON

  • Created by Gideon from harvested criminal souls (2023-02-16; 005 §Origin). Gideon “sends Citrine to gather criminals and outlaws,” then “harvests their souls to be converted into golems,” justifying it by sending the golems “to help people” and “progress society” (2023-02-16).
  • Locke’s unease: “Locke is a little creeped out by the golems for some reason” (2023-02-16) — the soul-harvest origin is the implied cause. CANON

Proto note

The vertical slice ships a golem only as a boss — the tomb-guardian Ossuary Sentinel (tomb_golem, 🗿) — not as rank-and-file. That proto golem is a tomb guardian / adaptive heavy and is not yet tied to Gideon’s soul-harvest origin above — the origin remains design lore (005 §Golems, Implemented golem boss note). See the Ossuary Sentinel page (best-a) for its stats and kit.

Design history

  • 2023-02-16 — golems appear in the test-setup enemy list; the same day Gideon’s soul-harvest golem economy is described and Locke’s unease around them noted. This remains the current lore.
