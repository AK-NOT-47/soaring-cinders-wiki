Enemy Design Philosophy

CANONCaelian OdysseyNotes / Design
Also known as: Scaling & Progression, Making Enemies Scary, Creature Design Philosophy
First conceived 2023-02-11 (M01498, FreshToast) · last in chat 2024-09-01 (M05683, FreshToast)

Enemy Design Philosophy CANON

The rules the whole bestiary obeys: enemies get harder through tactical complexity, not bigger numbers — you go from fighting bandits to a dragon, never from bandits to bandits with quadruple HP.

Summary — The bestiary is governed by a few finalized principles: (1) progression comes from tactical complexity, not HP bloat; (2) enemies vary in sensory gimmicks (blind, invisible, light-sensitive) so different dungeons reward different skills; (3) scary enemies are built through resource starvation, escalation, and phases that express their power, plus tapping relatable fears; and (4) classic creature traits are preserved (hydrae have loads of heads, griffons are bird-lions, dragons breathe fire). “Simple fantasy world upfront, weird stuff out back.”

Scaling & progression CANON

  • “Later enemies will not have bigger numbers than beginning enemies… They will rather be more complicated and tactical fights” — maybe some bigger numbers for balance, “but with reason” (005 §Scaling & Progression; the leveling/quality-system note).
  • Bandits → dragon, not bandits with quadruple HP: progression is meant to move from bandits to a dragon rather than “bandits to bandits with quadruple HP” (005 §Scaling & Progression). Backed by the linear-scaling decision: “Linear scaling over exponential scaling to limit number bloat and reduce numerical impact of later levels to emphasi[ze] the tactical depth of combat and equipment choice” (M02100, FreshToast, 2023-02-16).
  • Recycle minibosses to flatter the player: “you can throw what was once a miniboss at the player in a pair or trio to make the player feel cool” (M01498, FreshToast, 2023-02-11).
  • The strategic-progression arc is goblins → dragon → Lysander: the game teaches different strategic approaches through gameplay (Imps/Goblins → Vaenngrym → Lysander). “The best strat to beat goblins is raw individual overwhelming power” (005 §Combat Strategy).

Proto note: the “make fights end fast” instinct is implemented as the Escalation system (enemies gain an accuracy/crit “edge” the longer a fight runs) — an item owned by the systems batch. See Escalation Mechanic.

Enemy variety DEBATED

Discussed sensory gimmicks that let different dungeons incentivize different skills (M01448-region, CrunchyConniptions, 2023-02-11):

  • Some enemies are blind.
  • Some enemies are invisible and can only be seen with magic, but you can hear them.
  • Some enemies are sensitive to light and “will see your torch before you see them” — countered by a party member who sees in the dark without a torch (005 §Enemy Variety). See the lantern/light interaction.
  • “Different dungeons have levels and enemies that incentivize different skills to be used” (005 §Enemy Variety).

Making enemies scary

Techniques the team catalogued for frightening encounters (M01799–M01801, FreshToast, 2023-02-11):

  • Mechanically scary: resource starvation; extended conflict; events or phases that express the monster’s power (M01799).
  • Thematically scary: make it seem you only stand a chance because it’s weakened; have it kill something else scary; tap into common relatable fears — body horror, fear of heights, megalophobia, thalassophobia (M01800).
  • Herd instinct: “humans are herd animals. If the characters are scared — especially non-player characters like the villains — that’s a signal… for people to also feel apprehensive” (M01801).
  • Making a monster interesting needs “both writing experience… AND game design experience/creativity to make it work mechanically” (005 §Making Enemies Scary). These techniques are applied hardest to Vaenngrym (best-a) and its fear effect.

Creature design philosophy CANON

  • Consistent with the vision: “simple fantasy world upfront, weird stuff out back” (005 §Core Concept).
  • Keep classic traits intact: “Hydrae have loads of heads, griffons are bird-lions and dragons breath[e] fire” (M04623, FreshToast, 2023-10-24); “I think I want goblins as is, not changed much. There aren’t enough goblins out there” (M04618, CrunchyConniptions, 2023-10-24). See Imps and Hydrae/Griffons.

Tactical complexity (summary of the throughline)

  • Later enemies focus on tactical complexity rather than stat inflation, incentivizing different party compositions and strategies for different dungeons (005 §Tactical Complexity). This is the design intent that the proto’s Ossuary Sentinel boss enacts — beaten by reading intent, not out-damaging 132 HP.

Design history

  • 2023-02-11 — core scaling ideas: recycle minibosses in pairs/trios (M01498); the “make it scary” toolkit — resource starvation, phases, relatable fears, herd instinct (M01799–M01801); enemy-variety sensory gimmicks (blind/invisible/light-sensitive) (M01448-region).
  • 2023-02-16 — linear-over-exponential scaling locked to emphasize tactical depth (M02100, FreshToast).
  • 2023-10-24 — “keep classic traits intact” fixed as a creature-design principle (M04618, M04623).
  • 2024-09-01 — the ethos continues to guide new creature discussion (e.g. slimes following natural forms, M05683). These principles are recorded as finalized decisions (005 §Design Decisions Finalized).
