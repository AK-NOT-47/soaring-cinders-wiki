Enemy Intent Indicator System

Cross-IterationMixed
Also known as: Intent indicators, Foresight, telegraph system, Gambit indicator
First conceived 2023-02-09

Slay-the-Spire-style telegraphs that show what each enemy is about to do, so the player reacts with foreknowledge.

Summary — At the start of each round the game telegraphs every living enemy’s planned action; the player then reacts. The finalized design taxonomy is 23 indicators built from a handful of base types (Attack, Protect, Support, Disrupt, Gambit, plus Prepare and Unknown) and their dual combinations (2024-08-20). The proto ships a distillation — 6 readable intent categories — rather than the full 23-icon set (008 §7; proto src/core/combat/intents.ts). The idea began as a heavily-debated “Foresight” mechanic on 2023-02-09 and the exhaustive 23-indicator writeup was authored 2024-08-20.

Rules — implemented (proto)

(008 §7; proto src/core/combat/intents.ts, state.ts)

  • Every living enemy’s planned action is telegraphed at the start of each round (re-rolled each round) and the player reacts (declareEnemyIntents).
  • Ships 6 readable intent categories: Attack ⚔️, Dual ⚡ (a hit + a status), Disrupt 🌀, Support 🩹, Defend 🛡️, Gambit 🎲 (INTENT_CATEGORIES, classifyIntent).
  • Classification rules: finisher + attack → Gambit; attack + (negative status on foe OR a shove) → Dual; plain attack → Attack; status/shove only → Disrupt; heal → Support; shield/self-buff → Defend.
  • Enemy targeting is currently auto-chosen (lowest-HP, front-most) rather than fully telegraphed per-icon (chooseTarget).

The 23-indicator design taxonomy (design target)

design (2024-08-20; 008 §7):

Core indicators: Attack (shows physical vs magical) · Protect (mitigate incoming) · Support (heal / buff / cleanse) · Disrupt (afflict debuffs) · Unknown (uncategorizable / movement only) · Prepare (turn taken without action) · Gambit (the most deadly action of powerful enemies; almost always a dual indicator).

Dual indicators combine two of Attack / Protect / Support / Disrupt / Gambit; the icon takes the primary indicator’s appearance with added visuals for the secondary. Precedence: Gambit > Attack > Protect (Defend) > Disrupt/Support (which varies by case).

All 23: Physical Attack, Magical Attack, Phys/Magical Attack ×(Protect, Support, Disrupt), Protect, Protect/Support, Protect/Disrupt, Support, Support/Disrupt, Disrupt, Disrupt/Support, Gambit, Gambit/(Phys Attack, Mag Attack, Protect, Support, Disrupt), Prepare, Unknown = 23 total.

The settled comparison is ~10 more indicators than Slay the Spire (23 vs ~13) — StS doesn’t specify attack type or have a Gambit; StS changes its attack symbol by damage amount, “but we shouldn’t do that” (008 §7). (The in-session “only 5 more — no wait, 10 more” was an arithmetic self-correction, Part 23, not a design disagreement.)

Design notes & interaction

  • Unknown is deliberate: “Unknown indicator is fun. To keep a degree of unpredictability” — used sparingly, like when StS goes “???” (2024-08-20).
  • Conditional interruption: some actions are interruptible with a certain damage type or enough damage — “if a dragon’s about to breathe fire, it’s got flames flickering from the sides of its jaws”; deal enough damage to downgrade a scary Gambit (008 §7).
  • Player influence: players can interact with intents — “moving enemies around… forcing them into states where they must/want to change their action” (008 §7 Q&A). There are abilities that do extra damage to enemies that are going to attack (2023-02-10).
  • Gambit telegraphing: animations, special visual/sound effects, or a bark signalling “BIG THING COMING THIS TURN OR NEXT” (008 §7 Q&A).

Related systems

  • Distinct from Rank-Targeting / Combat-Readability UI — that is the player-side targeting feedback (a skill’s own from-rank/target-rank), whereas intent indicators are the enemy-side telegraph. The two are complementary and should not be duplicated (008 §7).
  • Intents interact with positioning (shoving enemies to force action changes) and the status bank (Disrupt/Support map to debuffs/buffs).

Open questions

  • Foresight vs tension. FreshToast initially preferred the tension of not knowing and worried telegraphing makes combat reactive rather than proactive, and easier; resolution: enemies signal future actions and the player is rewarded for reacting correctly — “let it simmer” (2023-02-09).
  • The proto’s 6-category distillation vs the 23-icon set: the 23 are the design target; GAME_DESIGN.md §5.4 lists them as content still to be authored (008 §7).

Design history

  • 2023-02-09 — original Foresight concept: a Slay-the-Spire-style mechanic where enemies signal their next move and the player attacks/defends/parries; heavily debated (FreshToast wary of it making combat reactive/easier), resolved as “enemies signal future actions and reward the player for reacting correctly,” left to simmer.
  • 2023-02-10 — early adjuncts: signals group into offensive/defensive/both; abilities that do extra damage to attacking enemies; “no purely self-defensive abilities except something like Thane’s taunt”; optional fade-on-their-own floating status-text reminders for accessibility (2023-02-10).
  • 2024-08-20 — the full 23-indicator taxonomy was authored: 7 base types, dual-indicator precedence rules, all 23 combinations, the ~10-more-than-StS comparison, and the “Unknown is fun” note. (This is the current design taxonomy above.)
  • Proto era — implemented as a 6-category distillation (Attack/Dual/Disrupt/Support/Defend/Gambit) re-rolled each round; the 23-icon set remains the target (008 §7, ).
