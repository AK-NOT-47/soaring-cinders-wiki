Progression & Difficulty

CANONCross-IterationMixed
Also known as: Difficulty System, Difficulty Scaling, Character Progression, Adjust Difficulty On The Go
First conceived 2021-05-25 · last updated 2024-05-19

Progression & Difficulty CANON

How the player grows over a run and how the game’s challenge is tuned — the settled position being that difficulty is adjusted on the go through in-game means, never chosen from a menu at the start.

Summary — Progression is deliberately bare-bones and level-gated, with no skill trees and no enemy scaling; the point of leveling is “the illusion of progress” because “the skill is what matters” (008 §9). Difficulty is built in as an inherent challenge, with in-game (not menu) tools letting the player nudge it up or down: the strong, settled design position is that a player should never pick a difficulty at the start of the game but instead choose challenge later, intuitively, as part of play (2024-05-19; 008 §16).

Character progression (design intent)

Progression uses a level-gated unlock cadence — no skill trees, no enemy scaling — where leveling delivers skills and traits rather than raw power. Leveling is “the illusion of progress”; “the skill is what matters” (008 §9 CANON).

Final structure (20 levels) (008 §9):

  • Level 1: 2 starting skills + base attributes.
  • Levels 2-20: technically +1 attribute every level; odd levels grant character skills, even levels grant passive traits.
  • 4 character-skill slots; end with ~8 skills (start with 2) and ~5 passive traits.
  • Linear (not exponential) scaling — HP and base damage rise only a little, to preserve tactical/equipment depth; enemies do NOT scale.

DEBATED Alternative structure (10 levels): 3 starter skills; levels 2-10 alternate trait/skill. The 20-level structure is the leading/final-favored version, with the 10-level variant as the alternative; exact HP numbers were never nailed down (008 §9).

SCRAPPED Skill trees are an explicit anti-feature — listed twice in the Part 3 “Fuck-this list” (“Fuck skill trees”) (2023-02-02; 008 §9). Any earlier XP-based / Skyrim-skill-tree / skill-points-per-level framing revived a source-rejected feature.

DEBATED design intent vs implemented: the proto implements no leveling at all — no XP, no level, no per-level unlock, no enemy scaling. Heroes ship with a fixed 4-skill kit and fixed stats (008 §9; proto src/core/combat/content.ts; GAME_DESIGN.md §14 “No leveling. Progression is equipment + skills only.”). The “no skill trees / no enemy scaling / linear scaling” intent is honoured; the level-gated cadence itself is unimplemented design intent.

Equipment progression (the Tier + Quality + Rune model) is a separate design and is not implemented in the proto (008 §9 Equipment Progression DEBATED). Run-level progression that IS implemented — degrading max-HP, healing jets, relics — lives in Shields & Wounded State and Treasure & Rewards.

Difficulty philosophy (current position)

The settled position, stated in full in 2024 (2024-05-19; 008 §16):

  • “Difficulty is weird. I think I have decided how to make that work.”
  • Challenge the player and encourage them to overcome the challenge — “so we make the game inherently challenging”.
  • But there are ways for the player to adjust that difficulty lower or higher through in-game stuff.
  • “I completely reject the idea of having the player select a difficulty in the beginning of the game. It makes no sense if you think about it. The player does not know what that choice means. Instead give them that choice later as part of the game in an intuitive manner that nudges them to take on challenges.” (2024-05-19)
  • TL;DR: “ALLOW PLAYER TO ADJUST DIFFICULTY ON THE GO.” (2024-05-19)
  • A YouTube video on difficulty in games was shared as “probably the best video I’ve seen that discusses difficulty” (2024-05-19).

Difficulty scaling (early framing)

EVOLVED The early scaling picture: early floors so easy people can solo them, growing more complex as you progress until reliance on co-op becomes central (Ghost of Tsushima nightmare-raids reference) (008 §13 Difficulty Scaling). This co-op-centric endpoint is superseded — the first game is single-player (Parts 9-24; see Game Type & Structure (Overview)).

In-game difficulty levers (design intent)

The “adjust on the go” philosophy is meant to be delivered through in-game systems rather than a menu:

  • Dynamic-difficulty challenges: areas/dungeons accessible early but challenging for that progression point — early = a reward for playing smartly, later = a return trip for a boost to tackle a greater challenge (2023-02-12; 008 §Difficulty Scaling reference).
  • PROPOSED Player-controlled dungeon challenge: a chalice-dungeon-style loop where “you make the dungeon harder, your reward is better,” the challenge level set by the player via a risk mechanic (2023-10-22; 008 §13 PROPOSED Chalice-Dungeon loop). Related to the DEBATED Risk/Reward Luck Bonus (008 §8).
  • Escalation — the per-fight Escalation Mechanic counter is part of the “end fast” combat philosophy, making individual fights deadlier the longer they run.

Music / progression motif

A thematic progression layer: “as you unlock more of the game… gather more of the great souls,” more instruments are unlocked, “eventually unlocking the voice at the end” — “all you need is one good track” (2021-07-01; 008 §9 Music/Progression System).

Open questions

  • DEBATED 20-level vs 10-level cadence, and exact HP numbers (008 §9).
  • DEBATED Whether the chalice-dungeon / risk-based difficulty lever is adopted, and how “time”/progress in a dungeon is measured for a luck bonus (008 §8, §13).

Design history

  • 2021-05-25 — Difficulty scaling first framed for the co-op vision: early floors solo-able, later floors require co-op (Ghost of Tsushima nightmare raids) (2021-05-25; 008 §13).
  • 2021-07-01 — Music-as-progression motif: unlock instruments/great souls, “eventually unlocking the voice at the end” (2021-07-01).
  • 2023-02-02 — Skill trees explicitly rejected on the “Fuck-this list” (2023-02-02) — forecloses any XP/skill-tree progression.
  • 2023-02-11 — A no-level “we don’t level up / no leveling as our identity” stance is floated, then left “pin on this / debate ongoing” (Part 6; 008 §9 EVOLVED).
  • 2023 (Part 8) — supersedes the no-level stance. The team re-adopts a bare-bones level system to deliver skills/traits (the 20-level or 10-level cadence), keeping the no-grind, no-enemy-scaling intent (008 §9 CANON, EVOLVED).
  • 2024-05-19 — current difficulty position. “Difficulty is weird… allow player to adjust difficulty on the go”; menu-select difficulty explicitly rejected (2024-05-19; 008 §16).
