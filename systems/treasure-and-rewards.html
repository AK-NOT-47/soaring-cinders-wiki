Treasure & Rewards

PROPOSEDCross-IterationMixed
Also known as: treasure hunting, boss rewards, performance-based rewards, reward kinds, loot rules, loot system, never lose found gear
First conceived 2021-02-16 (M00005, CrunchyConniptions) · last in chat 2023-10-22 (M04565, CrunchyConniptions)

Treasure & Rewards PROPOSED

How the game pays the player out: exploration-driven treasure hunting where effort equals value, performance-based boss rewards keyed to how well you fought, and a compact set of implemented run-reward kinds.

Summary — Rewards span three threads. Treasure hunting is a stated core pillar (“Treasure hunting is the best!”) built on the principle that the more effort and resources you sink in, the better the reward — hidden treasures most players never find are made especially valuable to reward exploration. Boss rewards are PROPOSED to be performance-based: players who do poorly get defensive items, those who do well get offensive items, the exceptional get cursed items — plus a boss-retry item to re-attempt for better loot. The playable proto ships a compact, fixed set of reward kinds at dungeon nodes. Finally, the CANON AoE-vs-multi-position bonus rewards hitting an enemy that fills multiple positions. Most higher-level reward logic is design intent; the reward kinds are implemented.

Treasure hunting

A stated core design pillar — “Treasure hunting is the best! Need game mechanics for this! Many!” (M00090, CrunchyConniptions, 2021-03-20; 008 §12).

Core philosophy — effort equals value:

  • “The longer it takes you to find a treasure, the better it is” (M00005, CrunchyConniptions, 2021-02-16), refined to “the more effort and resources you need to put in” (M00006, FreshToast, 2021-02-16).
  • A system that finds treasures on a map that most people don’t find and makes those especially valuable, to incentivize players to seek them out (M00007, CrunchyConniptions, 2021-02-18).

Treasure mechanics (008 §12):

  • Hidden treasures require exploration; clues and hints scattered through the world; puzzle-solving elements.
  • Environmental storytelling leads to treasures.
  • Rare treasures from difficult-to-reach locations.

This ethos feeds the dungeon-run loop’s harder-run-better-reward principle and the risk/reward luck bonus (M04565, CrunchyConniptions, 2023-10-22).

Performance-based boss rewards PROPOSED

On the first boss fight, a system identifies player skill and rewards accordingly (008 §13):

  • Players who do poorly: rewarded with HP and armor (defensive help).
  • Players who do well: rewarded with damage-dealing items (offensive).
  • Players who are exceptional: can receive cursed items.
  • Rationale: “Balancing the game for both low skill players and high skill players” (008 §13).

Boss retry PROPOSED

For every boss there is a corresponding item, found through exploration, that lets players retry that boss to get better rewards by performing better (008 §13). Ties the treasure-hunting thread back to boss rewards — explore to find the retry item, then re-clear the boss more skillfully for the higher tier of loot.

AoE vs multi-position bonus CANON

An AoE that hits an enemy which occupies multiple positions deals bonus damage — “as if hit twice but not double damage, maybe an extra 20%” (M01842, FreshToast, 2023-02-13; 008 §8; 000 lines 3141-3142). This is load-bearing for the Vaenngrym climax, where he fills all four positions, making an AoE hit on him meaningfully more rewarding. It is a combat-side reward rule tied to the positioning system (see the 2×3 grid / rank line).

Implemented reward kinds CANON · implemented

The proto grants a fixed set of reward kinds at dungeon nodes (008 §13; proto: src/core/campaign/types.ts RewardKind, run.ts applyReward):

  • relic — a named, non-duplicating collectible (e.g. “The Sunderer”, Ajax’s first relic).
  • maxhp — +4 base max HP to the whole party.
  • heal — a % of base max HP restored to all.
  • jet — +1 healing jet.
  • flask — +1 River Flask.

The proto grants these as fixed per-node rewards — it does not yet measure player skill or offer boss retries (008 §13, DEBATED design intent vs implemented).

Loot economy rules

Never lose found gear CANON · implemented

The oldest recorded loot decision, and still [IMPLEMENTED]: “you never lose items or equipment you find” (M00141, CrunchyConniptions, 2021-05-25; 006 §Loot System Notes). In code this holds — on defeat, the run routes to the lost phase but acquired relics persist on RunState.relics and are never dropped; only in-dungeon provisions / loose glint are at risk (proto src/core/campaign/run.ts). It rests on the provisioning/death rules: banked gold and found gear are safe; only what you brought and what you gained loose in the dungeon can be left behind (006 §Death Mechanics).

Gear acquisition constraints

  • Gear is not available for purchase, and gear never drops at Gold quality — Gold must be earned/found (see weapon drop rules).
  • Loot boxes are considered acceptable only for cosmetics / additional resources, “applied in a reasonable manner” — Apex/Overwatch cited as done-well examples (006 §Loot System Notes).

Open questions

  • DEBATED The performance-based boss reward tiers, cursed-item tier, and boss-retry items are design intent — the proto grants fixed per-node rewards and does not measure player skill (008 §13).
  • PROPOSED How “effort/resources put in” is actually measured to scale treasure value (M00006, FreshToast, 2021-02-16).

Design history

  • 2021-02-16 — original effort→reward principle: “the longer it takes… the better it is” (M00005, CrunchyConniptions, 2021-02-16), immediately refined to “the more effort and resources you need to put in” (M00006, FreshToast, 2021-02-16).
  • 2021-02-18 — extended into a find-the-hidden-and-make-it-valuable system to incentivize exploration (M00007, CrunchyConniptions, 2021-02-18).
  • 2021-03-20 — reaffirmed as a core pillar: “Treasure hunting is the best! Need game mechanics for this! Many!” (M00090, CrunchyConniptions, 2021-03-20).
  • 2021-05-25 — founding loot rule: “you never lose items or equipment you find” (M00141, CrunchyConniptions) — the oldest loot decision, never overturned.
  • 2023-02-13 — the AoE-vs-multi-position bonus (~+20%) established, load-bearing for the Vaenngrym climax (M01842, FreshToast, 2023-02-13).
  • 2023-10-22 — the treasure/effort ethos folded into the harder-dungeon-better-reward chalice loop: “you make the dungeon harder, your reward is better” (M04564, M04565, CrunchyConniptions, 2023-10-22). (See Dungeon-Run Loop.)
  • 2023-10-22 — Alternative Loot System (REJECTED): a checkpoint-gated moral-loot fork was proposed — skip the checkpoint → a cursed weapon that siphons the player’s life for a damage boost; play normally → a blessed weapon that heals (M04530–M04531, CrunchyConniptions). FreshToast rejected it: “I don’t think straight up discouraging interaction is going to get you the thing you want” (M04532, 2023-10-22). Only the checkpoint-gated delivery was rejected; the cursed/blessed weapon properties survive as weapon-design examples (see Weapons (Tiers, Types & Skills) / Equipment System (Tier · Quality · Rune)).
  • 2026-06-16 — implemented-code pass: proto ships fixed per-node reward kinds (relic/maxhp/heal/jet/flask) and the AoE-multi-position bonus; the performance-based boss tiers and boss-retry remain unimplemented design intent (008 §13; proto: src/core/campaign/*) — supersedes the performance-tier system as shipped behavior, though it remains the design target.
