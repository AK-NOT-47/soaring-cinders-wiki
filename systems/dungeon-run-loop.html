Dungeon-Run Loop

Caelian OdysseyMixed
Also known as: chalice dungeons, dungeoning, death-run, provisioning loop
First conceived 2023-10-22

The out-of-combat world loop: you enter a dungeon, push deeper through a chain of nodes and fights spending finite run resources, camp to reset your worn-down health, and either clear the boss or wipe — with harder self-selected runs paying out better rewards.

Summary — The dungeon run is the game’s repeatable core loop, framed by the team as “re-creating the chalice dungeons” of Bloodborne — repeatable dungeons you go through (the “act of dungeoning”) to grind materials for your weapon and better rewards (2023-10-22). Players are meant to have control over a dungeon’s challenge level via a risk mechanic — make it harder, get better loot (2023-10-22). Within a run you traverse a compass-navigated node graph, fight through encounters (some of which ambush you), spend limited run resources (Jets, the River Flask), watch your maximum HP ratchet down until you camp and rest, and — on defeat — get sent back to re-provision. Most of this is design intent; the vertical-slice proto ships a compressed, playable version.

The chalice-dungeon framing

The loop was crystallized on 2023-10-22 as a Bloodborne chalice-dungeon analogue — “similar, not the same”:

  • Repeatable dungeons you go through to grind materials to put into your weapon — though “grind is the wrong word,” it is the act of “dungeoning” (2023-10-22).
  • You are incentivized to do harder versions of the dungeons for better rewards — “you make the dungeon harder, your reward is better” (2023-10-22).
  • The player has control over the dungeon’s challenge level, replicable “through some kind of risk mechanic” (2023-10-22). Serves the same purpose as chalice dungeons without being identical.
  • Distinguished from Borderlands’ Mayhem mode, which is “just pumping numbers up which you eventually scale to match… not much of a difficulty slider” (008 §13).

This ties directly to the risk/reward luck bonus below, which is the leading candidate for the “risk mechanic.”

The run loop (structure)

The proto ships a single playable dungeon, “The Descent into Ajax’s Tomb” (the prologue), which demonstrates the intended shape of a run (008 §13; proto: src/core/campaign/content.ts):

start → fight → a branch (treasure path vs. ambush-elite path) → riverside camp → fight → boss

It is a compass-navigated node graph — never a spatial map (see Navigation Compass (Compass-over-Maps)). Each node offers next-node choices and may attach a fight, a reward, an ambush, or a camp.

Run resources

Resources are consumed over a run and are the currency of the risk decision — push deeper and you spend down your safety margin. values (008 §4; proto: src/core/campaign/run.ts):

  • Healing Jets: start a run with 2, cap 5; each jet heals one character for 60% of their base max HP.
  • River Flask: start with 1; heals the whole party for 35% of base max HP each use.
  • Relics: collected, named, non-duplicating (boss/treasure rewards — see Treasure & Rewards).
  • (Glint/gold and the wider provisioning economy from Part 17 are design intent — not modeled in the proto run state.)

Degrading maximum HP

The health system is what makes a run a war of attrition. Maximum healable HP (the ceiling) starts full and ratchets down as you take damage, until you rest — healing is a stop-gap, not full recovery, so characters get worn down across a run (008 §5, Part 18).

Proto implementation (src/core/campaign/run.ts):

  • Each won fight degrades every party member’s ceiling by 3 HP, down to a floor of 60% of base max (CEILING_DEGRADE_PER_FIGHT = 3, CEILING_FLOOR_RATIO = 0.6).
  • Current HP is clamped to the ceiling.
  • A knocked-out hero who survives a won fight is revived at 25% of ceiling so one bad fight doesn’t silently end a run (KO_RECOVER_RATIO).

This drives the whole run-loop tension: the further you push without resting, the lower your ceilings — which is exactly what the risk/reward bonus rewards.

Camp / rest

Resting is the pressure-release valve. rest at a camp restores the ceiling to full and heals to it (008 §5; proto: run.ts — rest). In the notes’ fuller (design-intent) framing, rest points also re-provision the party and bank any gold acquired (banked gold is never lost, protecting the economy) (008 §8, Part 17). The vertical-slice dungeon places a riverside camp mid-run between the two fight beats (008 §13).

Resting resets your accumulated risk — under the luck-bonus proposal, resting would zero out the bonus you’ve been building by pushing on.

Ambush

Some encounters ambush the party: an enemy makes a sound and rushes in, and if caught the party’s positioning scatters at the start of battle (008 §2, Part 11).

  • Proto: an ambush node flag triggers a Fisher–Yates shuffle of the heroes’ starting ranks before the fight is built (proto: src/core/campaign/run.ts — scatterFormation; ambush on DungeonNode in src/core/campaign/types.ts).
  • Darkness bands are modeled as a darkness?: number field intended to weight ambush rate — currently flavor in the proto (campaign/types.ts). Ambushes are used to “spook players in dark areas.”
  • Counter: attack the ambusher just as they reach you — a QTE element (“a bit meh”), used as a small, telegraphed minigame, sparingly (008 §2).
  • Players can use a consumable item to pre-emptively know whether they’ll be ambushed.
  • Locations are designed for it: open fields with charging bulls, foggy bogs with ninja frogs (008 §2).
  • Crossroads variation: an ambush might let Locke (or an enemy) take an extra turn (008 §2).

The vertical slice’s branch offers a treasure path vs. an ambush-elite path — the reward-vs-risk choice in miniature (008 §13).

Defeat: death-run & provisioning

The notes’ full death-run (Part 17, 2023-10-22): on defeat you are sent to the nearest rest point and must provision again (2023-10-22). “Like other corpse-run mechanics, it is grounded in another system: provisioning” (2023-10-22). Provisions and glint (gold) earned while exploring are left behind but can be reclaimed by defeating the enemy that beat you (slightly stronger the second time); defeated again before reclaiming, they’re lost. Compared to Lords of the Fallen (008 §8).

  • Loadout rule: weapons can be swapped to exploit enemy weaknesses, but sorceries/skills are fixed once slotted — a deliberate constraint to keep character-building meaningful; rapid Dark Souls-style gear-swapping was rejected as game-breaking (008 §8).

What the proto ships instead: a simpler defeat model — a party wipe sends the run to a lost state; there is no reclaim-from-the-enemy, no glint banking, and no provisioning economy in run state (proto: src/core/campaign/run.ts — absorbCombat defeat branch; RunState has no glint field). The softer analogue is the knocked-out-but-recovered rule above. The hard rule “never lose found items” is canon and honoured — items/relics are never stripped (008 §8, §1 Core Game Loop).

Risk/Reward luck bonus

The leading candidate for the chalice-dungeon “risk mechanic.” A proposed luck bonus that grows the longer a player goes in a dungeon without resting (better loot/rewards/multipliers), encouraging players to push their luck — modeled loosely on Lords of the Fallen’s multiplier and Darkest Dungeon’s light system (008 §8, Part 17).

  • Origin: “I think it’d be cool if the player was rewarded with some kinda luck bonus the longer they spent in a dungeon without resting” (2023-10-22). “I kinda want players to push their luck as much as possible” (2023-10-22).
  • Could be “some kind of scale, stat or whatever that is a number you can interact with” — via consumable items that give it a straight-up boost, or a passive (e.g. “Sable is naturally lucky and causes it to scale faster”) (2023-10-22).
  • Measuring “time” is the open problem: counting fights won punishes stealth; maybe rooms cleared instead (008 §8).
  • The luck bonus was chosen over baking difficulty into dungeon design because it is “much simpler to implement” (2023-10-22). Crunchy noted he liked the chalice-dungeon system but not the Lords-of-the-Fallen luck mechanic, and would “have a look at them again and get back to” the team (2023-10-22) — left undecided.

Open questions

  • How to measure “time in dungeon without resting” for the luck bonus — fights won (punishes stealth) vs. rooms cleared (008 §8).
  • Whether the luck bonus or design-baked dungeon difficulty is the right “risk mechanic”; left for Crunchy to revisit (2023-10-22).
  • Whether the full reclaim-your-provisions death-run ships, given the proto’s simpler lost-state model (008 §8).
  • The ambush-counter QTE (“a bit meh”) — kept sparingly or cut (008 §2).

Design history

  • 2021-02-16 — earliest seed of “reward for effort”: “The longer it takes you to find a treasure, the better it is” (2021-02-16), corrected to “the more effort and resources you need to put in” (2021-02-16). This effort→reward ethos later underpins the harder-run-better-reward loop. (See Treasure & Rewards.)
  • 2023-02-13 — AoE-vs-multi-position bonus rule established for combat rewards on packed enemies (2023-02-13). (See Treasure & Rewards.)
  • 2023-10-22 (morning) — the death-run / provisioning defeat loop proposed: defeat → nearest rest point → re-provision; reclaim left-behind provisions/glint by beating the enemy that beat you (2023-10-22). Crunchy: “I am happy with this. It fits in well with the provisioning system” (2023-10-22).
  • 2023-10-22 (morning) — the luck bonus proposed as the push-your-luck reward for going deep without resting (2023-10-22).
  • 2023-10-22 (midday) — the loop reframed explicitly as “re-creating the chalice dungeons”: repeatable dungeons for weapon materials, harder = better rewards, difficulty controlled by a risk mechanic (2023-10-22). The luck bonus chosen as the simpler risk mechanic; Crunchy left to reconcile it against the chalice system (2023-10-22) — current, unresolved.
  • 2026-06-16 — implemented-code pass: proto ships the compass node-graph run, ambush scatter, degrading ceilings, camp-rest, and run resources, but a simpler lost-state defeat rather than the reclaim death-run, and the risk/luck bonus is not coded (008 §13, §8; proto: src/core/campaign/*) — supersedes the reclaim death-run and luck bonus as shipped behavior, though both remain design targets.
