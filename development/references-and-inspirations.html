References and Inspirations

The consolidated pile of games, anime, films, mythology, music and educational content the team named as touchstones across all 24 parts of the design chat — with Dark Souls and Bloodborne as the acknowledged core.

Summary — Across three years of discussion the team cited dozens of works as inspiration. The stable core is Soulsborne — “Dark souls and bloodborne will always be the core to me (until some[one] makes a better game)” (2021-03-13) — layered with JRPG, tactics, and character-collection games (XCOM, Fire Emblem, Genshin/Pokémon), plus anime, film, mythology and a running soundtrack playlist. This page consolidates those references; individual mechanics they inspired live on the systems and Design Philosophy pages.

The core: Dark Souls & Bloodborne

The stated foundation. “Coop dark souls adventure with engaging combat” was the earliest clear gaming direction (2021-03-13), and “Dark souls and bloodborne will always be the core to me” fixed the north star (2021-03-13). Specific praise: Bloodborne’s “quality over quantity,” its thematic traps and weapon design (009 §Part 1); memorable FromSoft bosses — Sif, Artorias, Maria, Friede (009 §Part 1); Sekiro’s refusal to scale to skill (“The challenge forces you to improve on your own,” 009 §Part 1). Dark Souls also supplies the homey Firelink-Shrine bonfire feeling (see Design Philosophy) and the “crows” sacrifice-an-item mechanic (009 Appendix). Caveat noted early: “Dark Souls has a problem with lack of build diversity in built in progression” (2021-03-13).

Games (by role)

Souls-likes & action

  • Sekiro — best FromSoft bosses; grapple points in the environment; no skill-scaling (009 §Parts 1, 2).
  • Ashen, Absolver — souls-like references; Absolver’s “goldlinking” steal mechanic (009 §Part 2).
  • Lords of the Fallen — luck-multiplier system, “nerve-wracking” (009 §Part 17).
  • NieR: Automata — combat feel (009 §Part 1); progression “toned down” reference (009 §Part 1).
  • Ghostrunner, Titanfall 2 — grappling / movement-combat mixture; Titanfall 2’s grapple “loved” (009 §Part 2).
  • DMC, Bayonetta, Transformers Devastation, Metal Gear Rising: Revengeance — hack-and-slash / scoring / slicing feel (009 §Part 2).
  • Katana Zero, Hotline Miami, SuperHOT — one-hit-kill / puzzle-combat approaches (009 §Part 2).

JRPG, tactics & party

  • XCOM & D&D — position-system inspiration for the Combat Position System (009 §Part 20).
  • Fire Emblem (“Fremblem”) / Three Houses / Radiant Dawn — grid-based inspiration; Three Houses for NG+ and battalion-communication issues (009 §Parts 20, 21, 24).
  • Etrian Odyssey — similar to the 2×3 grid; Yu-Gi-Oh as a card-game analogue (009 §Part 20).
  • Slay the Spire & Into the Breach — deterministic, readable communication of outcomes (009 §Part 20).
  • Pokémon — “counts as JRPG”; nuzlockes and sentimental attachment; evolution-through-hardship analogy; exploration skills (Klee/Amber-style) (009 §Parts 11, 13).
  • Persona / SMT — fusion system; Persona 5 specifically for UI, portraits, animation, the interrogation-scene in-medias-res opening, and music (009 §Parts 13, 19).
  • Darkest Dungeon — cited as what not to do for positioning (“inferior imitation avoided”) and for frustrating unpreventable deaths, but also Death’s Door and its light system (009 §Parts 17, 20).
  • Golden Sun, Zelda / Zelda-likes, Xenoblade — dungeon-design research (Golden Sun “renowned for its dungeons”) (009 §Part 21).
  • Genshin Impact — character/gacha model, artifact system, exploration skills, progression (009 §Parts 1, 13, 17).
  • Valheim — progression system (009 §Part 1). Skyrim — exploration, compass-over-map navigation, skill trees, first-person horror (009 §Parts 1, 2). Divinity: Original Sin 2 — narrator, source-points/armor system, writing; also cited with problems (abuse cases, OP warfare) (009 §Parts 1, 2, 11, 13).

Other named games

  • Death Note (Light vs L narrative structure) (009 §Part 1); Ghost of Tsushima (nightmare-raid co-op) (009 §Part 1); Infamous: Second Son (neon powers) (009 §Part 1).
  • Fallout (Nuka Cola, radio, political messaging, treasure troves), Disco Elysium (readable adverts/books; explicitly an inspiration source), Helldivers 2 (parody propaganda) — all feeding the anachronism/immersion philosophy for Caelian Odyssey (009 §Part 24).
  • Hades / Cult of the Lamb — genre options for the Project Magitech concept (009 §Part 12).
  • Vampire the Masquerade — cited as what not to do (too-complex vampire org) (009 §Part 22).
  • Monetization exemplars — Apex Legends and Overwatch loot boxes “done well”; see Monetization & Business.

Anime, manga & film

  • Hunter x Hunter — narrators; the Phantom Troupe as a party-vs-party antagonist template (009 §Parts 2, 5).
  • Frieren — Himmel’s dungeon exploration reflecting JRPG habits; peaceful nostalgic tone (009 §Part 24).
  • Ranking of Kings, Bocchi the Rock — underdog storytelling “done right,” measured/personal cruelty (009 §Part 19).
  • My Hero Academia (All Might/Deku mentorship), Red Dead Redemption 2 (Dutch/Arthur mentor dynamic and gang decline) — mentor/protégé references (009 §Parts 17, 22).
  • Spider-Verse / Spider-Man — “great power, great responsibility”; a good version of the death/legacy theme (009 §Part 17).
  • Berserk (Guts’ Dragonslayer → Warpath weapon), Fate/Zero (what makes a king), Kaguya-sama, Sword Art Online — assorted character/scene references (009 §Parts 17, 19).
  • Game of Thrones — measured cruelty, reactions to cruelty, Jon Snow as underdog, and a debate on mature vs “too horny” sex handling (009 §Part 19).
  • Death Note, Albedo (Overlord), Gojo (JJK), Shadow the Hedgehog — character-personality / ability / “magical skates” references (009 §Part 2).

Mythology, real world & MTG

  • Celtic and Norse mythology — via Tolkien, as a source for creature names (goblins as house spirits, kobolds, hobs, bugbears) (009 §Part 17).
  • The Bible — Satan/fallen-angel and Raphael/angelic-purity framing for the character Raphael (009 §Part 20).
  • Real-world biomes — salt flats, thermal pools, coastal biomes, redwood forest, sinkhole forests, Garden of the Gods (from a TikTok level-design reference) (009 §Part 11).
  • Real-world flavor for Caelian Odyssey — golden-age pirate history, Caribbean climate/culture, the 18/1900s of “wacky invention,” 90s carbonated drinks and glass-bottle sodas (009 §Part 24).
  • Magic: The Gathering — an old MTG set referenced for feathered-dragon design in the Vaenngrym feather debate (009 §Part 11).

Educational & technical content

  • YouTube channels: Mark Brown’s Game Maker’s Toolkit (GMTK), Design Doc, Extra Credits (009 §Part 2).
  • GDC talks on characters, camera, AI, and enemy design; a ~1h40 gacha-RPG review using Genshin as a case study; a water-level design video (009 §Part 2).
  • Courses & communities: multiple Udemy courses for Unreal Engine and C++; r/unrealengine; r/gamedev legal resources (009 §Parts 2, 11).
  • Assets: Unreal marketplace (Frank Pack animations; a cel-shading asset “to rip apart and replicate”), Mixamo, and a 40GB Sonniss sound-effects pack (009 §Parts 2, 11).
  • Academic sources quoted on game design — functional vs aesthetic information; challenge and reward from “learning / using / using-in-extraordinary-situations” the mechanics (009 §Design Quotes from Academic Sources).

Soundtrack playlists

A running set of Spotify (and one YouTube) playlists shared as “soundtrack inspiration.” The first: “This is what I’ve got so far for what might be good soundtrack inspiration” (2021-08-03), with earlier YouTube playlists (2021-07-24) and many later ones through 2024 (2023-02-14 → 2024-03-30). FreshToast liked keeping each playlist “around 20 minutes” (2023-02-09). Also referenced: sea-shanty YouTube links and the Borderlands 1 opening song (009 §Part 1).

Open questions

  • References are descriptive touchstones, not commitments; several games are cited both as models and as “what not to do” (Darkest Dungeon, Vampire the Masquerade, Witcher 3), so a citation here does not imply the mechanic was adopted.

Design history

  • 2021-03-13 — Soulsborne fixed as the core direction: “Coop dark souls adventure” and “Dark souls and bloodborne will always be the core to me” (2021-03-13).
  • 2021-07 → 2021-08 — soundtrack-inspiration playlists begin (2021-07-24, 2021-08-03).
  • 2023 — the reference base broadens heavily into JRPG/tactics/character-collection games (Persona, Fire Emblem, Genshin, Pokémon, XCOM, Etrian Odyssey) as combat and party systems take shape (009 §Parts 11-22).
  • 2024-02-13 — dungeon-design research (Golden Sun, Zelda, Xenoblade; RPGFan dungeon article) is the latest major reference addition (2024-02-13); a further soundtrack-inspiration playlist is shared 2024-03-30 — the most recent reference on the page.
