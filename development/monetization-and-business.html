Monetization & Business

CANONCross-IterationNotes / Design

Monetization & Business CANON

The team’s stance on money: loot boxes are fine if reasonable and cosmetic, the game will likely ship under a UAE commercial license, and a realistic ship window is roughly two to three years of development.

Summary — The monetization posture is moderate: loot boxes and gacha-style elements are acceptable “applied in a reasonable manner” — cosmetics or extra resources — with Apex Legends and Overwatch cited as done-right examples (M00170–M00173, FreshToast, 2021-05-25). On the business side, the team looked into selling the game through a UAE commercial license (needed above AED 50,000 / ~£11,200 revenue, and untaxed there), noting the UK would tax it regardless (M03032–M03038, CrunchyConniptions, 2023-03-14). The working development timeline is “2 years to put things together” plus “another year for polish and last touches before launch” (M03044–M03046, 2023-03-14).

Monetization stance CANON

Loot boxes are not objected to in principle: “I don’t actually have much issue with loot boxes applied in a reasonable manner such as for earning cosmetic items or additional resources” (M00170, FreshToast, 2021-05-25). Cited exemplars of doing it well: “I think Apex Legends does it well” (M00171, FreshToast, 2021-05-25) and “Overwatch’s loot boxes were fine too, honestly” (M00173, FreshToast, 2021-05-25). Fortnite was raised with uncertainty (M00175, 2021-05-25) — “is it predatory? I hear epicgames are good natured” (M00177, CrunchyConniptions) — answered “it’s had its ups and downs” (M00179, FreshToast, 2021-05-25). Crunchy took the feedback on the gacha elements “into account” and resolved to use those titles as references (M00169, M00174, CrunchyConniptions, 2021-05-25).

This sits alongside a separate, mechanically-oriented anti-meta idea discussed the same day — “a system by which players are disincentivised to use meta builds/teams… ie points system where it is expensive to use the most popular characher, weapons, items” (M00172, CrunchyConniptions, 2021-05-25) — which is a balance concern rather than monetization, but was captured in the same exchange.

An early pricing thought-experiment framed the ambition: FreshToast wondered whether anyone would buy a hypothetical “$500 game up for sale on steam,” and Crunchy answered “what if it was skyrim times 10 with good gameplay and online coop,” “cross platform,” “epic narrative” (M00163–M00167, 2021-05-25). This is an aspirational pitch, not a real price point.

Legal & business CANON

The team investigated UAE commercialization. “In the UAE, you need a commercial license for… any revenue above AED 50,000” (M03032–M03033, CrunchyConniptions, 2023-03-14) — noted as “11,200 British pounds” (M03034, 2023-03-14). Crucially, “you don’t get taxed on it” in the UAE, whereas “in the uk you will get taxed on it regardless” (M03035–M03036, 2023-03-14). Conclusion: “so, we will likely sell the game through a commercial license” (M03037, 2023-03-14), with the team confident they were prepared — “Me and noods have looked into this heavily and are now more than prepared to do so” (M03038, CrunchyConniptions, 2023-03-14). A Reddit r/gamedev thread on legal needs and a game-dev legal guide were shared as resources (009 §Part 11 Resources; 009 §Part 12).

Development timeline CANON

The ship-window estimate below is the business framing; for the full project chronology see Development Timeline. Asked “When do you think we will have a game to sell? 3 years?” (M03040–M03041, CrunchyConniptions, 2023-03-14), the estimate landed at: “Idk, a year? 2 years? I was being generous with a year probably. I think 2 to 3 years is likely” (M03042–M03043, FreshToast, 2023-03-14), refined to “I think 2 years to put things together” (M03044, CrunchyConniptions) plus “another year for polish and last touches before launch” (M03046, CrunchyConniptions, 2023-03-14) — “It really depends on how intense the production stage will be” (M03045, FreshToast, 2023-03-14).

Related engine/timeline context: UE5 was judged “not production ready,” so unless building for two-plus years the team might start in UE4 and convert later — “Two years is the least that I was expecting” (009 §Part 2 Technical Development Notes).

Development & delegation philosophy

A later business-model stance (2024-05-18) frames the studio as delegation-first: “This is the future of digital work” (M05409, CrunchyConniptions, 2024-05-18) — piecemeal the project and delegate anything that can be done by anyone (M05410, 2024-05-18): “really at the core of it, we only need 1 thing[]: Game design” (M05414, CrunchyConniptions, 2024-05-18), with the code, audio, voice, animation and art delegated to freelancers (M05415, 2024-05-18). The Japanese industry (freelance 3D modelers and anime animators) is cited as the model (M05417, M05418, 2024-05-18), and “digital nomad” work is pitched as a team perk (M05419, 2024-05-18; 009 §Part 22 Freelance/Delegation Philosophy). Earlier, an Upwork freelancer (Jahongir Erkinov) had been shared as a concrete lead (M03076, CrunchyConniptions, 2023-05-16; 009 §Part 12).

Design history

  • 2021-05-25 — the monetization stance is set: loot boxes acceptable if reasonable/cosmetic, Apex & Overwatch as good examples, Fortnite left ambiguous; the “$500 game / Skyrim ×10” aspirational pitch is floated the same day (M00163–M00175, FreshToast/CrunchyConniptions, 2021-05-25).
  • 2023-03-14 — the business plan is worked out in one session: UAE commercial license above AED 50,000 (untaxed there, taxed in the UK), likely sell under that license, and a “2 years + 1 year polish” timeline (M03032–M03046, CrunchyConniptions/FreshToast, 2023-03-14) — the most detailed monetization/business plan.
  • 2024-05-18 — the delegation-first studio model is articulated: piecemeal the project, core members do game design while code/audio/voice/animation/art are freelanced, Japan cited as the model, digital-nomad work as a perk (M05409–M05419, CrunchyConniptions, 2024-05-18) — the most recent business-model statement.
