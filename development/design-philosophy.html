Design Philosophy

CANONCross-IterationNotes / Design
Also known as: Game Design Rules, Design Principles
First conceived 2021-05-13 (M00091, CrunchyConniptions) · last in chat 2024-02-13 (M05292, CrunchyConniptions)

Design Philosophy CANON

The design commandments the team returned to across three years of chat: no time limits, distrust of maps, mechanics that interact elegantly, and a homey, cozy game that is “not a hassle to pick up.”

Summary — The team’s design philosophy is a loose but stable set of rules and tastes, most first stated in 2021 and reaffirmed through 2024. The hard rules are “time limits are taboo” and (with dissent) “maps are taboo.” Beneath those sit a cluster of preferences: a game should feel homey and cozy; its mechanics should interact elegantly; challenge matters (a “git gud” leaning); a game is “a series of meaningful choices”; and puzzles should be “few, short and good only.” Most of these are aspirations rather than finalized mechanics.

The rules

Time limits are taboo CANON

The first stated design rule. “Time limits are frustrating and annoying”; “Time limits are not a fun mechanic. use other fun mechanics”; “Time is used for measuring. its very good for measuring. measuring is not fun.” The rule closes with a small manifesto: “Let there be chaos. let there be freedom. let there be comfort” (M00091, CrunchyConniptions, 2021-05-13; 000 §Rule 1; 008 §Rule 1). This is the design reason the dungeon runs measure progress by fights/rooms rather than a clock.

Maps are taboo DEBATED

The second rule, and the one that drew immediate pushback. “Maps are boring. Maps are tedious. Maps are effective and productive. Maps are not fun. Maps are for boring nerds” (M00092, CrunchyConniptions, 2021-05-13). FreshToast disagreed in the same minute — “I disagree about maps but there are pleasant alternatives” (M00093, 2021-05-13) — and the position softened into a nuanced one: maps “can be nessecary to travers giant worlds, but they are inherently not fun” (M00094), so “you would only use to guide people to the fun, not because MAPS are fun” (M00095, CrunchyConniptions, 2021-05-13). The REAL taboo was later clarified as the case where the world is too hard to traverse yourself so you must lean on a minimap; the dislike is “more about minimaps specifically” (000 §Rule 2). The preferred alternative is a compass over a map, Skyrim-style, with a very simple actual map (000 §Navigation Systems). Because it was never fully resolved, this rule is tagged DEBATED.

Preferences and taste

Make the game homey / cozy CANON

A recurring north star. “Make the game homey” — cozy, easy to pick up, “not a hassle to pick up” (M03691, CrunchyConniptions, 2023-09-26; 000 §Make the game homey; 009 Appendix). The touchstone is Dark Souls 1’s Firelink Shrine: “In dark souls 1, first bonfire gives you more estus flasks. I am now realizing I want that feeling. The homey feeling of firelink shrine” (M04097, CrunchyConniptions, 2023-10-13). The feeling is later summarized as “its familiar and homey” (M05292, CrunchyConniptions, 2024-02-13). The team also wants the home village to feel endearing so the player longs to return (009 §Part 19 Critical Reminders).

Mechanics should interact elegantly CANON

Stated flatly by spirit_noodle: “Mechanics should interact elegantly” (M02896, spirit_noodle, 2023-03-05; 000 §line 5138; 009 Appendix). The related deterministic-design principle — “Movement is both simple and perfectly deterministic (never random)” — comes out of the Combat Position System discussion: strategy should be easy to follow, always knowing where a character will go, “No guessing” (009 §Part 20). Chaos is welcome, but only “as result of unintended or exciting new interaction. Instead of confusion” (009 §Part 20). And no single mechanic should take over all strategy (009 §Part 22 Critical Reminders).

A game is a series of meaningful choices CANON

Stated as “A game is a series of meaningful choices” (M00246, CrunchyConniptions, 2021-07-03) — Crunchy’s rendering of the well-known Sid-Meier maxim — and endorsed by FreshToast, who noted the more famous version uses “interesting” but he preferred Crunchy’s word: “I’d replace ‘interesting’ with ‘meaningful’ but otherwise yes this is important” (M00247, FreshToast, 2021-07-03). Related early quotes from the same era: “Awesomeness is a byproduct, not the goal” (M00232, CrunchyConniptions, 2021-06-13, crediting “Lettuce”); “At essence of all games are like puzzles to solve to proceed”; “Video games should be full of ellipsis” (009 §Design Philosophy Quotes).

Challenge matters (“git gud”) CANON

On whether the developer should scaffold low-skill players, the team leaned toward Miyazaki: “I don’t think it’s the responsibility of the developer to foster skill in the player. Otherwise, you’re playing the game for them”; “maybe Miyazaki is right. just bully your players to ‘git gud’” (M00219, CrunchyConniptions, 2021-05-25). The escape hatch: “If challenge matters then yes, if not then let them overlevel like in a jrpg or whatever” (M00223, FreshToast, 2021-05-25) — answered simply, “Challenge matters!” (009 §Progression System Debates). Skill-based rewards were floated but judged “incredibly exploitable” and dropped in favor of just making rewards more numerous or higher quality (009 §Progression System Debates).

Puzzle philosophy PROPOSED

Puzzles should be “few, short and good only”; “fuck riddles.” A good puzzle works like a boss fight — introduce a mechanic, complicate it, then introduce something new or force the player to reinterpret it. The intent is light puzzling to set mood and break pacing: simple, unobtrusive, “make em think just a little” (009 Appendix, source 000 lines 2760-2774). Suggested placement structures: big exploration with a good puzzle at the end; a puzzle in the middle with a climactic fight; or an ongoing puzzle interacted with in brief chunks — roughly one puzzle per dungeon across ~7 dungeons (009 Appendix, source 000 lines 2775-2778).

Repeated dialogue breaks immersion PROPOSED

The aspiration to a game “where dialogue never repeats,” acknowledged as hard for talking shopkeepers. Dragon’s Dogma’s “They’re masterworks all” is cited as the good case; Bethesda / Starfield repetition as the painful one (“Bethesda doesn’t count. They put their voice talent through the meat grinder”) (009 Appendix, source 000 line 7232ff). Compare the later immersion-through-familiarity principle: references that “you just get it. No explanation. No lore dump” (009 §Part 24 Design Philosophy).

Death should be punishing but fair CANON

Death “has to be punishing, but not too punishing” — don’t lose everything, but give a chance to right a dumb mistake; don’t force the player to return or punish them for exploring; make them long for home in sentiment and return naturally during the story. The team wants players to “push their luck as much as possible,” with each player deciding how far to go (009 §Part 17 Design Philosophy).

The four pillars

When the team enumerated what the game is, they named four pillars — Narrative, Battle, Party Management, Exploration — alongside four “key aspects”: Narrative, Gameplay, Art direction, Sound design (& Music) (009 §Four Pillars, Part 8; cf. the party-management notes, M01422, spirit_noodle, 2023-02-11).

UX-polish appendix (aspirations) PROPOSED

Consolidated low-severity polish intents from across the notes (009 Appendix — Design Principles & UX Polish). Each is descriptive design intent, not a finalized mechanic:

  • Title screen must exist, look pretty with cool music, and say something like “Press any button to begin journey” (000 line 1607).
  • Character-selection feedback should be obvious — a drop-shadow glow/halo under the character, an eye-catching arrow, or a colour/glow change on the health bar (000 lines 1861-1866).
  • UI consistency: input, colour-coding, and abbreviations must be consistent AND explained; intuitive limits, rounded numbers, intelligent use of space (000 lines 3170-3172, 951-953).
  • Break-pots destructibles as you explore, “incredibly snappy in and out of combat” (000 lines 3757-3759).
  • The fountain could work like Dark Souls crows — sacrifice an item for a reward (000 line 5143).
  • Chests should be important (000 line 6161).
  • Coyote time as an “important addition to any fast-paced platformer movement system” — prevents the “feels bad” moment of falling off a platform edge (009 §Part 2 Coyote Time).

Open questions

  • DEBATED Whether maps are truly taboo or just minimaps; the compromise (“guide people to the fun”) was reached but never formalized (M00092–M00095, 2021-05-13; 000 §Rule 2).
  • DEBATED How exactly to handle maps vs navigation (009 §Part 1 Unresolved Questions).
  • PROPOSED The puzzle-per-dungeon count (~7) and placement structures are suggestions, not decided (009 Appendix).

Design history

  • 2021-05-13 — the first two design rules are laid down: Rule 1 “Time limits are taboo,” Rule 2 “Maps are taboo.” Maps is contested in the same conversation and immediately softened to “only to guide people to the fun” (M00091–M00095, CrunchyConniptions/FreshToast, 2021-05-13).
  • 2021-05-25 — the “git gud” / “Challenge matters!” stance settles the low-skill-player debate; skill-based rewards are dropped as exploitable (M00219, M00223, 2021-05-25).
  • 2021-06-13 → 2021-07-03 — the taste quotes accrue: “Awesomeness is a byproduct, not the goal” (M00232, 2021-06-13); then “A game is a series of meaningful choices” (M00246), endorsed by FreshToast who preferred it over the more famous “interesting” wording (M00247, 2021-07-03).
  • 2023-03-05 — “Mechanics should interact elegantly” articulated as a standalone principle (M02896, spirit_noodle, 2023-03-05).
  • 2023-09-26 → 2024-02-13 — the “homey / cozy” goal is named and repeatedly reaffirmed, anchored to Firelink Shrine (M03691, 2023-09-26; M04097, 2023-10-13; M05292, 2024-02-13) — the most recent restatement of the game’s felt goal.
