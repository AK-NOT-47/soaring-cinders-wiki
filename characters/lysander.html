Lysander

CANONSkyward CindersNotes / Design
Also known as: Evil Hero
First conceived 2023-02-09 · last updated 2024-08-28

Lysander CANON

The celebrated “golden hero” of Skyward Cinders and its central antagonist — a descendant of Ajax who made a pact with a daemon for power and doesn’t give a shit where that power comes from.

Summary — Lysander is the realm’s idolized hero, descended from Ajax, whose legendary strength was supposedly a gift from a benevolent otherworldly being — actually the result of a pact with an evil spirit, known only to Eris (2023-02-16). Built on family legacy rather than his own merits, he makes his own pact to gain power worthy of his ancestor, betrays and kills Eris, magically devours his own party members, and is finally killed by Locke Evertide (002 §Lysander). His relationship with Locke is “the key” of the story — a mentor-protégé dynamic modeled on Dutch & Arthur (RDR2) and All Might & Deku (MHA) (2024-05-18).

Appearance

  • Tall (002 §Lysander: Visual).
  • Purple-orange-yellow gradient irises like Vaenngrym — “sunset irises,” the reputed mark of Ajax’s descendants (2023-03-05).
  • In phase 2: sickly light pours from his mouth and eyes — bluish or indigo, per the visual language of warm/orange/good vs cool/blue/bad (2023-02-16).
  • Visual theme: “In my head he is the golden hero, the shining ray of hope” — though he doesn’t have to be literally gold (2023-02-16).

Personality

  • Initially appears as the golden hero, shining ray of hope (002 §Lysander).
  • “Doesn’t give shit about where power comes from” (2023-02-16).
  • Celebrity sellout — an intentional character flaw (002 §Lysander).
  • Concerned or confused in the prologue (002 §Lysander: Prologue).
  • Infuriated at Locke’s challenge; growls in a manner reminiscent of the dragon (2023-03-03).

Background & Motivations

  • Descendant of Ajax; has Ajax’s relic — possibly, or it was on Lysander the whole time and suppressed him without his knowledge, until it fell from him or was somehow broken (2023-03-03).
  • Built his reputation on family legacy rather than his own merits; felt pressure to live up to Ajax’s legend (002 §Lysander).
  • Made a pact with a daemon to gain power worthy of his ancestor (002 §Lysander).
  • Locke is a nobody/underdog partly because Lysander exists — even Thane Farrow begins more experienced than her (2023-11-16).
  • Like Gideon and other human characters, his cruelty comes mostly from human flaws — in contrast to Raphael, the story’s inhuman tragic monster (2024-02-10).

Motivations: live up to Ajax’s legendary status; gain power by any means necessary; maintain his “golden hero” image while pursuing dark power; surpass his ancestor’s achievements (002 §Lysander).

Goals: gather the chaos emeralds (ostensibly to defeat the dragon, actually for power); make a pact with the spirit for greater strength; become the strongest hero regardless of cost; maintain his public image while pursuing secret power (002 §Lysander).

Flaws: celebrity sellout — image over substance; no moral compass regarding the source of power; willing to sacrifice anyone including allies; arrogance blinds him to consequences; becomes consumed by power and loses his humanity; betrays those who trust him without hesitation (002 §Lysander).

Reputation & Unraveling Pace

  • People are meant to look up to Lysander and admire him as a hero — people idolize heroes, and he in particular is admired (2023-10-25).
  • He unravels much faster than Dutch from RDR2 — a deliberately imperfect comparison, since Dutch isn’t really an antagonist in RDR2 and that story is largely about the gang’s slow decline; here the seeds of doubt are immediate (2023-10-25).
  • His reputation lasts a little longer than the doubt, but devolves entirely by the time the dragon is dealt with at the end of Act 2 (2023-10-25).
  • Design rationale: “the audience doesn’t want to be duped — they want to feel smart” (2023-10-25).
  • He refuses to face the dragon down, entranced by the power of Ajax’s legacy, and lets towns burn while Locke rounds up help; the moment where Lysander hesitates and Locke steps up shifts public opinion in her favor and defines her as Ajax’s proper heir (2023-10-25).
  • The dragon highlights the three motivations: Ajax wants glory, Lysander wants strength, Locke wants to protect and make change — “Lysander is the failure of the three of them because he’s the only one who failed to step up when needed” (2023-10-25).

Abilities & Traits

  • Affinity: Electric (002 §Lysander).
  • Power involves soul and sacrifice; can grab someone by the head and immediately turn them to dust; grips allies causing them to die (2023-02-16).
  • Boss-fight gimmick: when he kills a party member he regains HP, gets a buff, and gains AP — this doubles as a teaching mechanic (2023-02-16).
  • Has a diluted version of Ajax’s power, which waned with each heir; to recover, he has to kill and drain the life of others (2023-02-21).
  • Only realizes his ability to take at the last moment — had only used it accidentally when young; enough to prove incredibly threatening to Locke in their final encounter (2023-02-21).
  • PROPOSED — not implemented Has skills that directly increase or scale based on Escalation Mechanic. (The escalation counter is implemented in the proto — src/core/combat/state.ts — but Lysander himself is unbuilt; the proto’s only boss is the Ossuary Sentinel tomb golem.) (002 §Lysander: Abilities)
  • Has developed his daemonic power but not his draconic heritage; has a hint of dragon-like aspects, far less clear than on the bastard Damaris (2023-02-22).
  • Can occasionally intuit the draconic language (2023-02-22).
  • Gets skills to interact with/consume “shadows” (the Shadow System’s magical traces of defeated characters) for strength (2023-02-19); scary-boss idea: 2nd-phase Lysander can break shields early or open with an attack that strips a shield (2023-02-19, 2023-02-20).

Relationships

  • Locke — the key relationship of the story; write Lysander and how Locke bounces off him (2024-05-18). Locke is in awe of him as her hero and nervous about how to address him (“sir”/“hero” rejected as cringe); the age gap is only about 4–5 years (2024-08-27). At the trial dungeon he considers the boss’s treasure the real prize, dismisses the Sunderer as worthless — should comment on it or attempt and fail to pick it up, mjolnir/excalibur style — while Locke gets the real prize all along: the spear plus the faith of the party members she saved (2024-08-27).
  • Eris — manipulated by her (initially); betrays and kills her (2023-02-16). The party tells Lysander about Eris in an effort to create division; he seems unphased, then for phase 2 “Lysander fucking magically eats his other party members” (2023-02-16).

Party members

  • Eris (member of party).
  • The Prideful Paladin (Thane’s Foil) — not in the hero party; independent (2023-02-16).
  • CANON The remaining 2 are a pair of orphaned, adoptive siblings — Flynt & Steele — largely loyal-to-a-fault, goofy pawns who work personally for Lysander (2023-02-16, 2023-06-04).

Role & Arc

Prologue: In the prologue dungeon looking for one of the chaos emeralds; it isn’t found but he kills some monsters; Lysander is concerned or confused. Lysander and Eris discuss gathering the chaos emeralds to defeat the dragon or something; Locke overhears (2023-02-19). He and Locke proceed to clear the dungeon; he misses out on the chaos emerald “because he’s dumb and misread the clue” (002 §Lysander: Prologue). The dungeon’s dead end is actually the secret entrance to Ajax’s Tomb; Lysander and Eris assume their intel about a relic in the cavern was false (2023-09-10).

Plot events: Makes a pact with the spirit for power. Eris finds out he doesn’t care about the demon powers and tells him about soul/sacrifice — Lysander interrupts by grabbing her by the head; she’s immediately dust (2023-02-16). The betrayal of Eris is the twist; could double-twist by making Eris seem like the obvious twist villain only to twist back to Lysander again (2023-02-16).

Boss fight:

  • Phase 1: the party faces Lysander and his party members. Eris, manipulating things to acquire Ajax’s power or make her own pact with the same spirit, is betrayed and slain by Lysander. Splash image: one hand still gripping the dusted robe of Eris hanging off skeletal remains, the other gripping one of his 2 allies, now dying; the other crawling towards the POV desperate to escape (2023-02-16). A second variation of the full-screen art comes with the sound effect of a body dropping; Lysander, faced away, half turns, revealing light pouring from his mouth and eyes (002 §Lysander: Boss Fight).
  • Phase 2: the party faces a buffed Lysander with his last compatriot on the ground trying to crawl away, weak enough to kill with a couple of attacks. If the player doesn’t attack them, Lysander takes the first turn to devour them, gaining AP and a buff for 2 turns; if the player kills them, Lysander gets no buff or AP but the player has used a couple of turns (002 §Lysander: Boss Fight).
  • Phase 2 scene: Lysander’s 2nd phase starts — Locke is doomed, humanity is doomed — but Ajax prepared for this moment, leaving behind a relic that had helped him contain his own power. The relic is with Locke or another character (a guardian to be created) — or it was on Lysander the whole time. A silhouette of Ajax appears: “Weary warriors, a curse of sorrowed days past stands in front of you but with the relic of old at hand, the curses power can be defeated. Now, stand tall and fight.” Locke speaks directly to Lysander: “Your light dims, Lysander… {she then looks up fiercely} Let it fade away.” Lysander, infuriated, growls in a manner reminiscent of the dragon; the fight begins (2023-03-03).

Final moments: Phase 2 ends with Lysander killed by Locke in suitably dramatic fashion. He maybe says “Why settle for only twice as bright?” — a reference to the family motto (2023-02-16).

Strategic progression arc: the best strat to beat the goblins is raw individual overwhelming power; the best strat to beat the dragon is to become as goblin; the best strat to beat Lysander is to become as dragon (2024-02-16).

Key quotes & notes

  • “Why settle for only twice as bright?” (possible final line) — the family motto is “Burn twice as bright, half as long,” and because of the deal it’s literal: Ajax shortened his lifespan via the pact, so Lysander will also die young (2023-02-16).
  • Anachronistic elements: does a cringe, parody radio advertisement for some cheap citrus soda sold in glass bottles — “It’d be funny probably and associates him with bad role models and celebrity pandering from our modern society”; he can’t even stand the mango-flavoured fizzy drinks but has to grin and bear it when offered one in public. Theme: commercialization of heroes (2024-08-28).
  • References: his second phase reminds of Godfrey, the First Elden Lord (Elden Ring) (2023-02-16). “Lysander” is the most recent name for the hero (2023-02-16).

Open questions

  • DEBATED How the pact revelation lands on him: a dud revelation that emboldens him (“Oh I have demon blood powers? Sweet!”), or he knew all along, or reverse manipulation / smart in secret / plays along anyway; gets a 3rd-act breakdown (2023-02-16).
  • DEBATED Where Ajax’s containing relic is: with Locke, with a guardian character yet to be created, or on Lysander the whole time, suppressing him unknowingly until broken (2023-03-03).
  • DEBATED Staging of Eris’s death: head-grab-to-dust vs. an alternate where, about to be defeated, he looks at his sword, says “It sings to me,” and plunges it into Eris (2023-02-16).

Design history

  • 2023-02-09 — original concept, unnamed: “Evil hero - challenges Locke’s idea of heroism and success,” listed with the first antagonist roster.
  • 2023-02-16 — named Lysander (“our most recent name for the hero”); descended from Ajax, whose strength was really a pact with an evil spirit known only to Eris. Same session: he betrays and dusts Eris; phase-2 devouring of his own party; soul-devour boss gimmick; the motto “Burn twice as bright, half as long” made literal — he will die young; phase-2 light fixed as bluish/indigo, retracting an earlier “green”; Godfrey (Elden Ring) phase-2 reference. Alternate “It sings to me” sword staging floated — unresolved.
  • 2023-02-16 — revelation-handling debate: dud revelation / knew all along / smart in secret — still open.
  • 2023-02-19/20 — prologue chaos-emerald impetus sketched, with Locke overhearing Lysander and Eris; Shadow System consumption skills for Lysander; shield-strip opener for hard dungeons.
  • 2023-02-21/22 — power lore: diluted take power waning with each heir; realizes his ability only at the last moment; daemonic power developed but not draconic heritage; occasionally intuits draconic.
  • 2023-03-03 — full phase-2 scene script: Ajax’s relic, the silhouette’s speech, Locke’s “Your light dims” line.
  • 2023-03-05 — sunset irises like Vaenngrym established for Ajax’s descendants.
  • 2023-09-10 — prologue dungeon tied to Ajax’s Tomb and the Sunderer; Lysander and Eris assume their relic intel was false.
  • 2023-10-25 — Reputation & Unraveling Pace settled: unravels much faster than Dutch, immediate seeds of doubt, reputation gone by end of Act 2; refuses to face the dragon while Locke steps up.
  • 2023-11-16 — Locke’s underdog framing built partly on Lysander’s existence.
  • 2024-02-10 — thematic contrast fixed: Lysander’s cruelty is human flaw, vs. Raphael’s inhuman evil.
  • 2024-05-18 — the Lysander–Locke relationship named as the key; Dutch/Arthur and All Might/Deku as dynamic models.
  • 2024-08-27 — Sunderer-dismissal beat (“somewhat important”); Sunderer as symbol of Locke’s independence from him; age gap set at ~4–5 years.
  • 2024-08-28 — cringe citrus-soda radio advert pitched — matches the current presentation above.
