Rune System

Skyward CindersNotes / Design
First conceived 2023-02-11

Materia-like slots on weapons and armour; every rune carries an upside and a downside, so building is optimization, not best-in-slot.

Summary — Runes are optional inserts that slot into weapon and armour rune slots (like materia). The defining rule is that all runes have both an upside and a downside, so choosing runes is a matter of what to sacrifice rather than simply slotting the strongest option. Because a base weapon with no rune is still fine (if sub-optimal), runes stay optional and add build depth that grows as the player finds more of them (2023-02-11).

Rules / Effects

  • Slots — weapons and armour can carry rune slots (2023-02-11). This should all apply to armour as well (2023-02-11).
  • Salvage — if a weapon drops with a rune already slotted but the weapon itself is bad, the player can dismantle that weapon for its rune.
  • Two-sided by design — every rune has an upside and a downside, making it “a matter of optimisation and not just slotting the best runes” — a choice of what to sacrifice.
  • Optional — since a downside-free base weapon still works, runes are optional; they let the player tweak a weapon to their preferences. In FreshToast’s words: “It also makes runes kinda optional since a base weapon with no downside will still be fine, if sub-optimal” (2023-02-11).
  • Slot count scales — higher Quality and Tier weapons might have more rune slots.
  • Described as a material-like system with upsides and downsides that adds a fun layer of complexity as you find more and build your own kit (006 §Rune System).

Rune examples

Illustrative runes, each showing the upside/downside pattern (2023-02-11):

  • Chance to poison on hit, but reduces base damage.
  • Increases base damage, but reduces crit chance.
  • Increases base damage, but makes the wielder take more damage in return.
