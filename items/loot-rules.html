Loot Rules (Found Gear, Rewards & Treasure)

CANONSkyward CindersProto-Implemented
Also known as: never lose found gear, loot system
First conceived 2021-05-25 (M00141, CrunchyConniptions) · last in chat 2023-10-22 (M04532, FreshToast)

Loot Rules (Found Gear, Rewards & Treasure) CANON · partly IMPLEMENTED

The one hard promise of the loot economy: you never lose the gear you find. Only what you carry into a dungeon is ever at risk.

Summary — The foundational loot rule is “you never lose items or equipment you find” (M00141, CrunchyConniptions, 2021-05-25) — one of the oldest recorded design decisions and still [IMPLEMENTED] in the proto. This sits on top of the provisioning/death system, where only in-dungeon provisions and loose glint are at risk on defeat. Treasure hunting is a first-class pillar (“Treasure hunting is the best! Need game mechanics for this! Many!”), and a punish-you-for-interacting alternative loot system was explicitly rejected (see Design history).

Rules / Effects

Never lose found gear CANON · IMPLEMENTED

You never lose items or equipment you find (M00141, CrunchyConniptions, 2021-05-25; 006 §Loot System Notes). In code this holds: on defeat, absorbCombat routes the run to the lost phase, but acquired relics are kept on the persistent RunState and never dropped — only in-dungeon provisions / loose glint are at risk (006 §Loot System Notes; proto src/core/campaign/run.ts). Found relics live on RunState.relics and persist across nodes. This is grounded in the provisioning/death rules: banked gold and found gear are safe; only what you brought and what you gained loose in the dungeon can be left behind (006 §Death Mechanics).

Reward kinds [IMPLEMENTED]

Treasure/encounter rewards are a small typed set (006 §Loot System Notes; proto run.ts applyReward, types.ts Reward):

  • relic — a named permanent relic (e.g. the Sunderer); deduped so it is never granted twice.
  • jet — adds healing jets (clamped to the 5-jet cap).
  • flask — adds River Flask charges.
  • maxhp — permanently raises the whole party’s max HP (e.g. “Trial of Nerve … +4 max HP each” for surviving the ambush narrows).
  • heal — a percentage party heal of base max HP, clamped to the current ceiling (e.g. “Quiet Moment … Heal 40%”).

Treasure hunting CANON

“Treasure hunting is the best! Need game mechanics for this! Many!” (006 §Treasure Hunting). Principles:

  • The longer it takes to find a treasure, the better it is — corrected to “the more effort and resources you need to put in.”
  • A system that surfaces treasures most players don’t find, and makes those especially valuable, to incentivize finding them (006 §Treasure Hunting).

Gear acquisition constraints

  • Gear is not available for purchase, and gear never drops at Gold quality — Gold must be earned/found (see weapon drop rules).
  • Loot boxes are considered acceptable only for cosmetics / additional resources, “applied in a reasonable manner” — Apex/Overwatch cited as done-well examples (006 §Loot System Notes).

Open questions

  • DEBATED What specific treasure-hunting mechanics to implement (006 §Unresolved Questions).

Design history

  • 2021-05-25 — the founding rule is set: “you never lose items or equipment you find” (M00141, CrunchyConniptions) — the oldest loot decision, never overturned.
  • 2023-10-22 — Alternative Loot System (REJECTED): CrunchyConniptions proposed a checkpoint-gated moral-loot fork — finish a dungeon without interacting with the checkpoint → a cursed weapon that siphons life from the player but gives a significant damage boost; do it normally → a blessed weapon that heals the player (M04530–M04531, CrunchyConniptions). FreshToast rejected it: “I don’t think straight up discouraging interaction is going to get you the thing you want” (M04532) — you could just give all players access to both and get the same effect, and it would suck for a player to get an item that doesn’t suit their playstyle (006 §Alternative Loot System (Rejected)). Not adopted; the risk/no-rest incentive was instead handled by the Luck Bonus system.
  • 2026-06-16 — Proto reconciliation: the never-lose rule and the typed reward set (relic/jet/flask/maxhp/heal) are [IMPLEMENTED] in the vertical slice (006 §Loot System Notes).

Related but owned elsewhere: the cursed/blessed weapon properties themselves survive as legitimate weapon-design examples (cursed weapons drain life for high stats; blessed weapons heal) — see Weapons (Tiers, Types & Skills) / Equipment System (Tier · Quality · Rune). It was only the checkpoint-gated delivery mechanism that was rejected.
