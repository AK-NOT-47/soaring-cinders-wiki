Dragon’s Blood DEBATED

The blood of the slain dragon — offered to Locke as a story choice after the kill, and the source of Ajax’s oldest lie.

Summary — Upon killing Vaenngrym, Locke Evertide is given the opportunity to drink the dragon’s blood (M02661, FreshToast, 2023-02-21). It is framed as a narrative choice, a moment of levity after the fight, that informs Locke’s opinion on Lysander’s pursuit of power and her understanding of Ajax. Whether it should carry a mechanical effect is unresolved — hence status: DEBATED.

Description

Dragon’s blood ties directly into Ajax’s mythology: Ajax’s “cover” is that he got his strength from drinking dragon’s blood after wounding one — though this is actually the first instance of Ajax using his power to drain life from the dragon during battle (M02651, FreshToast, 2023-02-21). Offering the blood to Locke echoes that legend and lets the player reflect on it.

Rules / Effects

  • The choice — after killing Vaenngrym, Locke either:
    • drinks the blood, everybody is grossed out, and nothing happens; or
    • finds solace in her own strength and refuses to partake (M02662, FreshToast, 2023-02-21).
  • Consequence — the Daemon of Want’s dialogue changes based on this choice (M02663, FreshToast, 2023-02-21).
  • Mechanical effect — DEBATED “Perhaps a mechanical change in the fight as well?” (M02664, FreshToast, 2023-02-21).

Open questions

  • DEBATED Whether drinking the blood has any mechanical bearing. The 006 Design Note argues against a purely cosmetic choice: “No mechanical change is extremely unsatisfying when compared to the contrary scenario. No need for this. It’s just confusing and serves nothing” (006 §Design Note) — i.e. either give it real consequences or cut the fake choice. Unsettled.

Design history

  • 2023-02-21 — introduced alongside Ajax’s cover story: Ajax pretends his strength came from drinking dragon’s blood, masking his life-drain power (M02651, FreshToast). The same evening it becomes a post-Vaenngrym player choice with no mechanical bearing, purely informing Locke’s view of Lysander and Ajax (M02661–M02662); the Daemon of Want reacts to the choice (M02663); a mechanical effect is floated but left open (M02664).
  • Doc-era note — a later design note pushes back on the “no mechanical change” framing as unsatisfying and pointless, flagging the choice for reconciliation (006 §Design Note).
