Prologue & Tutorial — The Descent into Ajax’s Tomb CANON

The opening dungeon run: Locke Evertide descends into a dark cave with Lysander and Eris, loses the sword he lends her, and pulls Ajax’s spear from the rubble on her own — the game’s thesis compressed into its first hour. Built and playable in the proto.

Summary — The prologue begins in medias res, Locke and Lysander already adventuring, then rewinds to a proper intro (004 §Prologue Structure). In a dark cave lit by Locke’s Lantern, Lysander lends Locke his backup sword (“A hero always looks out for his companions”), the party clears monsters, and Locke falls to a lower level where the borrowed sword shatters on a stone golem. She frees the rusty Sunderer (Ajax’s spear) herself, reads the golem’s weakness Lysander missed, and the run ends not at a dead end but at the secret threshold to Ajax’s tomb. The whole sequence is implemented and playable in the proto as the vertical-slice dungeon “The Descent into Ajax’s Tomb” (proto: src/core/campaign/content.ts).

Structure & beats

Opening (004 §Prologue Structure):

  • Begin in medias res, Locke and Lysander already adventuring together; at the end, rewind to the proper intro (M05004, FreshToast, 2023-11-16 notes the in-medias-res, start-you-off-screwed intent).
  • Start with Lysander’s party minus Eris, plus Locke, in a dark cave, the Lantern carried by Locke (M02442, CrunchyConniptions, 2023-02-19).
  • Lysander: “Watch the shadows. They hide just out of sight.” A monster pounces; he kills it in one hit (M02442, CrunchyConniptions, 2023-02-19).
  • Lysander is in the prologue dungeon looking for a chaos emerald; it isn’t found — he is dumb and misread the clue, while Locke is smart and reads it well (M02445, M02449, 2023-02-19).

Tutorial sequence (004 §Sequence Overview): Lysander center-frame, flanked by Locke & Eris; Locke armed with a sword. Show off Lysander’s power by protecting Locke from a creature and killing it without mercy. Enter, tease the reason for entering, tutorial battle begins. Locke laments her lack of reach and strength. Her sword is lost or broken at the battle’s conclusion. Combat is broken up by a puzzle emphasizing Locke’s competence; an ambush separates Locke from the others; she stumbles into and acquires the Sunderer, arms herself, and reunites for another battle. Post-battle she finds the way forward and is commended by Eris. Stealth & light mechanics introduced; final climactic battle tests the player’s basic knowledge (004 §Sequence Overview).

Outcome (004 §Tutorial Outcome): The Sunderer (first of Ajax’s seven relics) is acquired in a trial passed by Locke. What they reach is not a dead end but a secret entrance to Ajax’s tomb; Lysander and Eris assume the intel about a relic here was false.

Key moments (first-draft screenplay) CANON

  1. Sword Given — Lysander gives Locke his backup sword: “A hero always looks out for his companions.” Symbolic: the power he gives her (004 §1 Sword Given).
  2. Lysander’s Heroics — effortlessly dispatches monsters: “Ha! Is that all you’ve got?” / “Evil falls before the righteous!” Party hangs back (004 §2).
  3. Locke Falls — notices a faint GLIMMER in the rubble; the floor CRUMBLES; she falls into darkness (004 §3).
  4. Sword Breaks — the sword SHATTERS against a golem’s stone body (“Symbolic: power he gave her breaks”). She spots a rusty spear with a GLIMMER and pulls it free (the Sunderer) (004 §4).
  5. Weakness Discovery — “Wait… their joints! There!” She strikes the golem’s knee joint and defeats both golems with precise attacks (004 §5).
  6. Boss Fight — Locke fights off small monsters and saves the healers; Lysander charges the boss (“Cover me!”); Locke clears enemies first, then follows; Lysander delivers the final blow (004 §6).
  7. Treasure vs Spear — Lysander goes for the boss’s treasure, barely glancing at Locke’s spear: “Interesting weapon. Scavenging suits you.” Contrast: he thinks the treasure is the real prize, but Locke got the real prize — the spear and the faith of the party (004 §7).

Symbolism (004 §Symbolism Identified): Sword = power Lysander gave her (breaks); Spear = power she sought and developed. “Spear also represents her independence from Lysander” — “Only for him to want that same power for himself.” Foreshadowing: first command she follows forward; second command (follow to boss) she disobeys — “FORESHADOWING!” (004 §Foreshadowing).

Character notes: Lysander “kind of sounds like All Might”; the writers wanted his words overly heroic, his actions heroic but questionable. The spear was meant to read as a footnote that turns out to be the best find (004 §Character Notes; §Lysander Noticing Sunderer).

Proto implementation CANON — IMPLEMENTED

Built and playable as “The Descent into Ajax’s Tomb” (proto: src/core/campaign/content.ts). The graph realizes the beats almost exactly:

  • Start node “The Spare Sword” — Lysander’s “A hero always looks out for his companions” pressing his backup blade into Locke’s hands before the stair drops her into the dark.
  • Treasure node “Glimmer in the Rubble” — the borrowed sword shatters on a stone-cored foe; Locke pulls the rusted spear free, granted as { kind: 'relic', label: 'The Sunderer' }, “Ajax’s spear — the first relic.”
  • The misread clue is implemented as the carved line “the joint, not the wall” — Locke reads the golem’s weakness while Lysander skims past.
  • Boss: the Ossuary Sentinel, a tomb golem with an adaptive Adamant Shell defense (proto: src/core/combat/content.ts), guarding the threshold to Ajax’s tomb.
  • Outro confirms the “sealed door [bearing] a sun worn almost smooth … a single sunset-colored gem set where an eye would be … never a dead end … a threshold.”
  • Locke’s spear finisher Emberlance — “Ajax’s fire runs down the spear” — is implemented (proto: src/core/combat/content.ts).

This is the Act I “be overwhelming” combat lesson made playable (the golem’s joints, not the wall); see The Combat-Strategy Arc (Goblins → Dragon → Lysander → the Wish).
