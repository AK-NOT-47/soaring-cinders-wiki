Two-Path System (Undying vs Heroes)

Early ConceptNotes / Design
Also known as: Undying/Heroes, Undying vs Heroes, sun-bros structure
First conceived 2021-06-26

Two-Path System (Undying vs Heroes)

An early two-role structure — you play either an Undying (souls-like, revives forever, weak, borrows power) or a True Hero/Heroine (one life, far stronger, no do-overs) — later set aside for the single-player JRPG that Soaring Cinders became.

Summary — This is the game’s earliest conceived narrative structure, brainstormed in June 2021. The player would choose to be one of two kinds: an Undying, who always comes back after defeat and grinds each fight to victory, invading and syphoning power from heroes; or a True Hero/Heroine, who dies for good, carries their failures, and has far better stats. The two sides were “always fight[ing] each other,” and the structure carried an online/co-op (“invade or be sun-bros”) and gacha framing. The whole Undying/Heroes vision — and the gacha “Storyline Expansion” attached to it — is preserved here as an early concept, not the current design (004 §Main Narrative Structures); Soaring Cinders is now a single-player JRPG with Locke Evertide as protagonist.

Description

The Undying Path

  • You always come back to life after defeat (souls-like). (2021-06-26)
  • Keep repeating each fight until you reach victory.
  • Weaker than heroes, but if determined enough, the Undying eventually wear the heroes out.
  • Must invade heroes and borrow their power — syphoning strength from them.
  • Example: an Undying facing a boss weak to fire can invade a hero with fire-magic affinity and borrow that power “till they die and lose it (similar to embers in Dark Souls 3).”
  • Lore framing: “Undying are told to end the age of light to save their own kind.” (2021-06-26)
  • The Undying as “an ancient eternal species that was kept down by the heroes’ species … shriveled jerky due to the eons of suffering.” (2021-06-26)

The True Hero/Heroine Path

  • You do not come back to life; if you fail, you carry that failure with you.
  • Far better stats than the Undying.
  • The storyline incorporates the person’s failures and victories — failure and victory decide the storyline.
  • “Doesn’t mean the game or story is worse just cuz you lose, but there is great incentive to achieve victory.”
  • Lore framing: “Heroes are told to prolong the age of light to continue the prosperity of their own kind.”

Heroes Route ideas

  • Constant failure eventually leads to another NPC becoming the hero, who gets stronger and more hero-like the more you fail. Eventually the end boss is fought not by you, but by the NPC now regarded as the true hero/heroine.
  • Joke ending: if you do not damage a single boss at all, you lose your woman/man to another man/woman (the true hero/heroine, maybe) “in a cruel (and hilarious) manner.”

Major Boss scenario (the 75% choice)

A first encounter with a Major Boss (maybe the end boss) at 75% power — the player can finish him here (2021-06-26):

  • Option 1: finish him now → unlock the true ending where you fight the “TRUE END BOSS.”
  • Option 2: let him escape → he devours the “TRUE END BOSS” himself to become the true end boss.
  • Intent: “Hopefully this will lead to intense situation when one would decide to continue the pursuit of victory and delay this fight for later when one is ready.”

Relationship between the two sides

The two sides could be enemies or allies (2021-06-26):

  • “Undying be enemies to heroes … or … undying and heroes unit for a common purpose. Invade each other, or be sun-bros.”
  • “The invasion and enemies option seems more fun, but the unity option is interesting.”
  • Framed as four final outcomes that collapse into two: “let there be light or no light.”

Gacha / F2P layer (abandoned)

Attached to this structure was a gacha model, tagged in the doc (004 §Storyline Expansion):

  • “The heroes will follow the gacha model. New heroes will be added with their own storylines.”
  • The Undying take “the F2P approach”: to experience a true hero’s power, they must invade and borrow it.
  • Using the most powerful/popular hero makes you more likely to be invaded and have your power stolen — disincentivising everyone from just using the best heroes.
  • The Undying counterparts of heroes are bad at borrowing other heroes’ powers, while the original Undying is very good at it. (2021-06-26)

Open questions

  • Whether the two sides are enemies or allies (“invade each other, or be sun-bros”) was left genuinely open at the time — “the invasion and enemies option seems more fun, but the unity option is interesting”. Moot now that the structure is superseded.
  • Completing the story as a hero would “unlock the undying version of that hero which is … complicated and unclear” — the writer flagged it as unresolved.

Gameplay lineage — what the system seeded

Although the whole structure was set aside, two of its mechanics threads carried forward into the shipped single-player design (008 §10):

  • Invasion → anti-meta. The “invade and borrow a hero’s power” loop — and the rule that using the most powerful/popular hero makes you more likely to be invaded and robbed — is the seed of the later invasion-based anti-meta (2021-06-26).
  • Borrowing → Locke’s leadership. The Undying’s defining trait, borrowing others’ powers (with the note that the original Undying is uniquely good at it while hero-derived Undying are poor at it), later informs Locke’s “leader who borrows his allies’ skills” design; see the Reserve Skills / Borrow system (2021-06-26; 008 §5).
  • Hero unlocking. Completing the story as a hero would unlock that hero’s Undying version — “abandoned heroes” who retain aspects of their power — versus normal Undying who “were not originally heroes but just normal people”. Flagged unresolved at the time (see Open questions).

Design history

  • 2021-06-26 — original concept: the full Undying-vs-True-Hero two-path structure — revive-and-grind Undying who invade/syphon hero power vs one-life, high-stat heroes; the Heroes-route “another NPC becomes the hero” idea; the joke “lose your partner” ending; and the 75%-power Major Boss choice.
  • 2021-06-26 — expanded same day: the “age of light” faction framing (Undying end it / Heroes prolong it), the four-outcomes-into-two structure, and the “enemies vs sun-bros” relationship question.
  • 2021-06-26 — expanded same day: ending themes tied to regret (see Endings & Relationship Options), the “shriveled jerky” Undying-species idea, and the gacha/F2P monetisation layer with invasion-driven power theft.
  • Later (undated set-aside) — the entire Undying/Heroes two-path + invasion/co-op + gacha framing was set aside for the single-player JRPG direction (see Main Story Outline); the doc marks it and preserves it as history, not current design (004 §Main Narrative Structures). Supersedes all of the above.
