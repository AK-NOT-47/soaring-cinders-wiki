Lysander Arc-Pacing (Story Side)

Skyward CindersNotes / Design
Also known as: Lysander unravelling pace, seeds of doubt pacing
First conceived 2023-10-25

How fast the audience is meant to see through Lysander: the seeds of doubt are immediate, his heroic reputation lasts only a little longer, and he should unravel entirely by the time the dragon is dealt with at the end of Act 2 — much faster than a slow-burn like RDR2’s Dutch.

Summary — This page covers the pacing of Lysander’s villain reveal from the writing/story side (the combat-and-boss side lives in Main Story Outline and on Lysander’s own page). The team’s principle is that “the audience doesn’t want to be duped; they want to feel smart” (2023-10-25). Applied to Lysander: seeds of doubt are immediate (2023-10-25); his reputation lingers a bit longer but “likely devolve[s] entirely by the time the dragon is dealt with at the end of act 2” (2023-10-25). The Dutch/RDR2 comparison is acknowledged as imperfect.

The design principle

  • “The audience doesn’t want to be duped. They want to feel smart.” (2023-10-25)
  • The Locke/Lysander contrast established from the very start “works great for that” — the writer wanted Lysander to “unravel faster” (2023-10-25).

The pacing target

  • Seeds of doubt: immediate. (2023-10-25) The prologue’s “questionable but heroic” Lysander already plants them (see Main Story Outline §Prologue).
  • Reputation: “will probably last a little longer but likely devolve entirely by the time the dragon is dealt with at the end of act 2.” (2023-10-25)
  • The unravelling is dramatised at the Act 2 dragon climax: Lysander refuses to face Vaenngrym and lets towns burn while Locke rounds up help and grounds the dragon — the moment that defines her as Ajax’s proper heir and “shifts public opinion in favour of Locke and out of favour of Lysander” (2023-10-25). See Main Story Outline §Act II.

Why not a slow-burn (the Dutch/RDR2 caveat)

The Dutch-van-der-Linde comparison is judged imperfect (2023-10-25):

  • “If you’re gonna compare him to Dutch it’s not a particularly good comparison since they only share the one similarity.”
  • “Dutch isn’t really an antagonist in RDR2 and the story is largely about Arthur and the gang’s slow decline” — a different structural job than Lysander’s.
  • The reasoning ties back to the principle: “You’d get bored if the main bad guy was presented as the main bad guy but were also constantly being told ‘Oh no way, he’d NEVER be bad or wrong uwu.’” (2023-10-25) — the audience wants to feel smart, not duped.

Related design principle

“I like when different potentiates for a role have opposing views on the role’s purpose and how to succeed in it — especially if it’s a more nuanced, open-ended question which the audience can consider and answer themselves.” (2023-10-25). Lysander (might/glory) vs Locke (protect/change) is exactly this kind of opposed-view pairing.

Design history

  • 2023-10-25 — the whole arc-pacing exchange: the “feel smart, not duped” principle, the immediate seeds of doubt, the “devolve entirely by end of Act 2” target, and the Dutch/RDR2 caveat. This is a single-session decision with no earlier superseded version.
