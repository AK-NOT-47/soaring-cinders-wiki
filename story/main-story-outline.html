Main Story Outline

CANONSkyward CindersMixed
Also known as: Main Story Arc, JRPG Concept, Storyline Expansion
First conceived 2023-02-02 · last updated 2023-02-22

Main Story Outline CANON

The current single-player JRPG spine of Soaring Cinders: a party of “loser nerds” chases Ajax’s seven relics, Locke Evertide rises as the true heir while the celebrated hero Lysander rots into the very monster his bloodline hid, and the real endgame antagonist is the Daemon of Want — a wish you cannot stab.

Summary — This is the outline of the game as most recently conceived: a three-act JRPG (2023-02-19) that opens in medias res with Locke and Lysander already adventuring, delivers a playable prologue/tutorial (“The Descent into Ajax’s Tomb,” CANON — IMPLEMENTED IN PROTO) in which Locke acquires the Sunderer, and follows the party’s hunt for Ajax’s seven relics / chaos emeralds. Across the acts, Locke grows into the proper heir while Lysander decays into a soul-devouring, dragon-blooded final boss; defeating him is not the last beat — the true ending is the Daemon of Want. This spine supersedes the earlier Undying/Heroes two-path structure.

Core story (the JRPG concept)

  • A group of “loser nerds” who seek better in life and strive together; theme of vanity. (2023-02-02)
  • Initial goal: a special relic to improve themselves. They realise growth comes from within, abandon the relic, and instead pursue the “loser hero.” (2023-02-02)
  • “Keep it light, keep it humorous, don’t take it too serious.” (2023-02-02) — tone reference: Mob Psycho (004 §Tone Reference); character-arc reference: Denji from Chainsaw Man.
  • Antagonist archetype: “carried by OP magical gift from birth, always takes easy way out, always cheats.” (2023-02-02) — realised as Lysander.

Protagonist identity SCRAPPED male-isekai fork

Switching the main character to a male “isekai protagonist” was suggested during a party-order discussion and rejected (004 §Protagonist Identity). “Your weebness is showing again calling the main character an isekai protagonist.” (2023-02-14). Locke remains the protagonist. Kept only as a record so it isn’t re-proposed.

Narrative structure

  • The narrative is split into 3 named acts (names TBD); cities are split into sub-sections. (2023-02-19)

Prologue & tutorial — “The Descent into Ajax’s Tomb” CANON — IMPLEMENTED IN PROTO

The opening begins in medias res with Locke and Lysander already adventuring (Lysander’s party minus Eris), then rewinds to a proper intro (004 §Prologue Structure). Lysander’s line “Watch the shadows. They hide just out of sight.” opens the cave (2023-02-19). Key tutorial beats (004 §Tutorial Sequence, §Tutorial Scene - First Draft):

  • Sword given. Lysander hands Locke his backup sword — “A hero always looks out for his companions” — symbolising the power he gives her.
  • Locke falls. She spots a glimmer, the floor crumbles, and she is separated from the party.
  • Sword breaks. The borrowed sword shatters on a stone golem — the power he gave her breaks — and Locke pulls a rusty spear (the Sunderer, first of Ajax’s seven relics) free.
  • Weakness discovery. “Wait… their joints! There!” — Locke reads the golem’s weakness Lysander misread; the clue is implemented as “the joint, not the wall.”
  • Boss fight against the tomb golem (proto: the Ossuary Sentinel, with an adaptive Adamant Shell defence), guarding what is not a dead end but the secret entrance to Ajax’s tomb.
  • Treasure vs spear. Lysander eyes the boss’s treasure, barely glancing at Locke’s spear: “Interesting weapon. Scavenging suits you.” The contrast: he thinks the treasure is the real prize; Locke got the real prize (the spear + the party’s faith).

This entire prologue is built and playable in the proto (proto: src/core/campaign/content.ts, src/core/combat/content.ts), including Locke’s spear finisher Emberlance — “Ajax’s fire runs down the spear” (004 §Tutorial Outcome). Symbolism: sword = power Lysander gave her (breaks); spear = power she sought and developed herself, and her independence from him — “only for him to want that same power for himself” (004 §Symbolism Identified).

Main quest — the seven relics / chaos emeralds

  • The party needs a compelling, plot-central reason to collect the chaos emeralds; a threat (demons, a dragon) drives it. Lysander and Eris discuss gathering them to defeat the dragon; Locke overhears and decides to gather them herself, first to prove worthy, then for herself, finally to keep them away from Lysander — her motives shift from hero-pleasing to people-saving. (004 §Chaos Emeralds Quest)
  • The relics are keepsakes tied to Ajax and the spirit he made a pact with. Brought together they grant not superpowers but a revelation about Ajax and his bloodline. They were meant to be “somewhat of a dud in the end” — a deliberate anti-climax. (2023-02-11; 004 §Relic Details)
  • The relics as a “leash” CANON: gathering all seven makes one “inherit Ajax’s legacy” — which people misread as acquiring his power, when the act of acquiring is what makes one powerful. Ajax’s legacy is the final task: to challenge his descendant and prevent them from consuming thousands in pursuit of power (000 notes, Ajax’s Legacy System). The seven relics are the leash used to bind Lysander’s hunger in the phase-2 finale. (004 §7 Relics System)
  • See Chaos Emeralds (Ajax’s Seven Relics) (items) and Ajax for the relic system and Ajax’s Daemon-of-Want pact.

Act II climax — the dragon confrontation

  • Lysander refuses to face the dragon, ever more entranced by Ajax’s legacy, and lets towns burn. Locke upstages him: she rounds up help, works with various people to ground the dragon, and enters the den to face Vaenngrym. (004 §Dragon Confrontation)
  • The dragon exposes each man’s motivation — Ajax wants glory, Lysander wants strength, Locke wants to protect and make change — and marks Lysander as the failure who did not step up. The moment defines Locke as the proper heir, shifts public opinion toward her, and is the climax of her personal arc. (004 §Character Motivations Revealed)
  • Dragon’s Blood Choice CANON: on killing Vaenngrym, Locke may drink the dragon’s blood (a beat of levity) — either she drinks it, everyone is grossed out, and nothing happens, or she finds solace in her own strength and refuses. No mechanical bearing, but it informs her view of Lysander’s power-chase and changes the Daemon of Want’s later dialogue. Lore tie-in: Ajax’s cover story was that he gained strength from drinking a wounded dragon’s blood. (004 §Dragon’s Blood Choice)

Act III — Lysander’s villain turn & the two-phase final boss CANON

  • Lysander grows too strong for Eris to handle and has a 3rd-act breakdown: he doesn’t care where his power comes from (“Oh I have demon-blood powers? Sweet!”) and doubles down, wanting to kill and sacrifice the party, then do the same to everyone else. (004 §Lysander’s 3rd-Act Villain Turn)
  • Eris twist: Eris tells him about the soul/sacrifice power; he interrupts by grabbing her head and she is instantly dust, kicking off the breakdown. (see Eris’s arc)
  • Phase 1 — the radiant himbo hero: fights spectacularly, telegraphs his glory, refills his fading light by leeching the crowd; counter is to cut off the leech. His soul-devour gimmick: killing a party member restores his HP, buffs him, and grants AP.
  • Phase 2 — the curse made flesh: scales surface and the pact, starved of strangers, eats its host — he becomes the dragon his bloodline hid. Mechanic: a weakened compatriot is left crawling; if the player does not kill them, Lysander spends his first turn devouring them (AP + a 2-turn buff); if the player kills them first, he gets nothing but the player loses turns. The seven relics act as Ajax’s pre-prepared leash. Locke ends him: “Your light dims, Lysander… Let it fade away.” (004 §Lysander 2nd Phase Fight Scene). Final line: “Why settle for only twice as bright?” — a twist on the “burn twice as bright, half as long” family motto. (2023-02-16)

True ending — the Daemon of Want CANON

Defeating Lysander is not the last beat. The real endgame antagonist is the Daemon of Want — the spirit behind the whole bloodline curse, formed from the people’s rampant, diffuse desire for a savior (2023-02-21). It is exceptionally powerful, self-serving rather than evil, and stays hidden as the secret superboss. You cannot stab a wish: it is beaten by refusal — a people who stop wishing for a savior and grow their own light — leaving it to thin and starve (2023-02-21). See Daemon of Want and Endings & Relationship Options.

Story-through-gameplay — combat-strategy arc CANON

The combat-strategy arc is deliberately the same arc as the narrative; the player’s hands learn the theme before the cutscenes spell it out (004 §Combat Strategy Arc):

  • Act I — goblins & beetles: small swarming foes → be overwhelming (the “joints, not the wall” golem lesson is implemented in the prologue).
  • Act II — the dragon: a force of nature you cannot out-stat → become as a goblin (many, united, humble).
  • Act III — Lysander: the two-phase final boss → become as the dragon.
  • The true superboss, the Daemon of Want, cannot be cut at all — beaten by refusal.
  • The full arc: goblins → dragon → Lysander → the wish itself. (“Best strat to beat goblins is raw individual overwhelming power / to beat dragon is to become as goblin / to beat Lysander is to become as dragon.”)

Design history

  • 2021-06-26 — before this spine existed, the game’s structure was the Undying-vs-Heroes two-path system with a gacha layer. Later set aside for the single-player JRPG below.
  • 2023-02-02 — JRPG concept established: “group of loser nerds,” theme of vanity, abandon-the-relic growth arc, cheating OP-gifted antagonist, Mob Psycho tone. Supersedes the two-path structure.
  • 2023-02-11 — the seven emeralds framed as a deliberate “dud”/revelation, not a power payoff.
  • 2023-02-14 — male-isekai-protagonist fork proposed and rejected; Locke stays the lead.
  • 2023-02-16 — Lysander’s finale line “Why settle for only twice as bright?” coined.
  • 2023-02-19 — three-named-acts structure decided; prologue cave dialogue drafted.
  • 2023-02-21 → 02-22 — the Daemon of Want established as the pact-giver and hidden true superboss; “practically immortal so long as desire remains to feed it”. Latest layer of the current outline.
  • 2026-06-16 (proto reconciliation) — the prologue is confirmed built and playable in the proto (“The Descent into Ajax’s Tomb”), and the combat-strategy-arc-to-act mapping and Daemon true-ending framing are synthesised from the GameBible (004 header; proto: src/core/campaign/content.ts, gamebible/data/story.js).
