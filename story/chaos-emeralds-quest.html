The Chaos Emeralds Quest (Ajax's Relics)

CANONSkyward CindersMixed
Also known as: Ajax's Relics, The 7 Relics, The Seven Chaos Emeralds, Ajax's Seven Thingies
First conceived 2023-02-09 · last updated 2023-02-26

The Chaos Emeralds Quest (Ajax’s Relics) CANON

The main-quest collectathon: seven relics of the hero Ajax, framed to the party (and the world) as a power-grab, but actually a leash forged to stop his bloodline from becoming monsters.

Summary — The spine of the main storyline is the pursuit of the seven chaos emeralds — properly Ajax’s seven relics. Gathered, they do not grant superpowers; they deliver a revelation about Ajax and his bloodline, and function as the leash the ancient hero left to bind his own hungering descendants (Lysander chief among them). The collection was deliberately designed to be “somewhat of a dud in the end” — an anti-climax by intent, not a botched payoff (2023-02-11). The player’s real reward is the act of gathering itself, which is what makes the bearer strong.

Description

The relics are the questline’s throughline, driving Locke’s arc from hero-worship to independence (see the motivation chain in §Why the player collects them below). They are treated as valuable and sentimental to Ajax, tied to the spirit he made a pact with (004 §Relic Details).

  • Relics are actually valuable and need a purpose — not filler loot (004 §Relic Details).
  • They should be sentimental to Ajax and tied to “his whole deal”; an object he owned that was shattered, related to the spirit he made his pact with (004 §Relic Details).
  • Brought together they grant a revelation, not superpowers — a way to learn about Ajax and his bloodline (004 §Relic Details).

The first relic, the Sunderer (Ajax’s spear), is acquired in the prologue tutorial — the first of “Ajax’s seven thingies” (004 §Tutorial Outcome).

Rules / Effects — the “leash” CANON

The 7 relics form an informal set of trials. Once all are acquired, the bearer will “inherit Ajax’s legacy.” People misinterpret this as acquiring Ajax’s power; in truth, the act of acquiring them is what makes one powerful (004 §7 Relics System).

Ajax’s legacy is specifically a final task: to challenge his descendant and prevent them from consuming thousands in pursuit of power (000 notes, Ajax’s Legacy System; 004 §7 Relics System). Ajax “left behind a relic that had helped him contain his own power in the past” (004 §Lysander 2nd Phase Fight Scene). The proto GameBible synthesizes these into a single reading: the seven relics are the leash — keepsakes bound to the spirit Ajax bargained with, scattered as trials so that when one of his blood grows monstrous, someone clever and patient enough can gather them and bind the hunger. The climactic use of “the relic of old” against Lysander’s phase 2 is therefore the purpose the relics were forged for (proto: gamebible/data/story.js, gamebible/data/quests.js; grounded in 000 notes).

Why the player collects them (in-world impetus)

A compelling, plot-centric reason is required for the player to gather them (2023-02-19). The chain settled on:

  1. A threat looms (maybe demons coming, or a dragon); a hero must grow stronger and sends the player to collect (004 §Core Concept).
  2. Lysander and Eris discuss gathering the emeralds to defeat the dragon (2023-02-19).
  3. Locke Evertide overhears and decides to gather them herself to prove worthy (004 §Core Concept).
  4. Her motive shifts from pleasing the hero to saving the people; she finally works to keep them away from Lysander (004 §Core Concept).

The deliberate “dud” CANON

The seven emeralds “were meant to be somewhat of a dud in the end” — a deliberate anti-climax rather than a payoff (2023-02-11). This is consistent with the relic concept: gathered, they grant a revelation, not superpowers. The design intent is that the dud is the point — a subversion of the collectathon-reward expectation. (Cross-ref: this “dud revelation” attitude mirrors Lysander’s own reaction to learning his power’s source — “Oh I have demon blood powers? Sweet!” (2023-02-16).)

Design history

  • 2023-02-09 — a chaos emerald first appears concretely: Lyra the Necromancer “has one of the seven chaos emeralds” (2023-02-09), establishing the seven-emerald set as scattered plot MacGuffins.
  • 2023-02-11 — FreshToast recalls the emeralds “were meant to be somewhat of a dud in the end,” locking the anti-climax intent while arguing to keep ordinary loot/rarity separate so players still have a reason to upgrade gear (2023-02-11).
  • 2023-02-19 — the in-world impetus is worked out: a plot-centric reason to collect them; Lysander & Eris seek them to defeat the dragon while Locke overhears and pursues her own path (2023-02-19).
  • 2023-02-21 — the relics are tied to Ajax’s pact with the Daemon of Want (2023-02-21), grounding the “leash”/legacy reading.
  • 2023-02-26 — a land-pirate NPC is floated to inform Locke of family history and where a chaos emerald is (2023-02-26).
  • 2026-06-16 — proto reconciliation folds the notes-grounded “legacy = final task to challenge the descendant” and “relic that contained his power” into the single relics-as-leash framing (proto GameBible).
