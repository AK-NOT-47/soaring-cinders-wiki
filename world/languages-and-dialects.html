Imperial Lycosian

Imperial, or High Lycosian, is the most common Lycosian dialect throughout the Lycosian Empire. It is nicknamed the Trade Tongue because it is the dialect usually learned by foreign merchants and politicians.

As a meta-language guide, Imperial Lycosian is comparable to several forms of real British English, especially northern English accents. It serves as the base for most character dialogue in Caelian Odyssey. Proclitic contractions and archaic phrasing help make the world feel foreign while remaining comprehensible.

Defining features

  • Frequent proclitics: “it is,” “to dance,” and “is quiet” can become “'tis,” “t’dance,” and “'squiet.” These are less common outside colloquial speech.
  • The letter R is left unstressed, especially at the end of words. All Lycosian dialects except Northern Lycosian are non-rhotic.
  • Liberal use of schwa, with little distinction between schwa and strut vowels.

Other Lycosian dialects

  • Northern Lycosian: spoken across Sylmir and northern regions. It has a harsh sound, rhotic R, and esoteric vocabulary. It is infamously difficult to understand.
  • Southern Lycosian: spoken among communities of Thera, especially in southern Lycosia. It originated from the language of the former southern kingdoms annexed by Lycosia.
  • Racket: an offshoot of Imperial, primarily spoken by mercenaries in Fiore and Lycosia.

Northern and Southern Lycosian are comparable to Irish/Scottish and Welsh accents respectively. Racket combines northern English slang and Italian-English accent influence. Imperial vocabulary and phrases remain common among these sister dialects.

Imperial vocabulary and phrasing

Notable words

  • Calyx: an enchanted object’s core magical component, typically where the enchantment’s glyph is located.
  • Sorcery: a tradition of structured magic based on meditative breathing; also a spell performed through sorcery.

Archaisms

Real English archaisms used in Imperial Lycosian include: Anodyne, Belike, Craven, Curative, Hence, Hither, Host, Nary, Rive, Shrive, Thence, Thither, Whence, and Whither.

Substitute words

  • Nigh: may replace “almost,” “near,” and “nearly,” though usage is less consistent in casual speech.
  • Afore: replaces “before” in many contexts; “before” is still spoken but used exclusively as a preposition.
  • Farewell: used instead of “goodbye” and considered suitable for formal and informal speech.
  • 'Tis: one of several “it” contractions, alongside forms such as “'twould.”
  • Belike: replaces “probably” and usually appears at the end of a sentence.
  • This day: replaces “today”; people refer to day and night by their beginning, such as “morning” or “nightfall.”
  • Morrow: replaces “tomorrow” and may also replace “morning” when referring to the next morning. “Overmorrow” means the day after morrow.

Phrases

  • By Her name: an oath with religious connotation, meant to carry more weight than a simple promise.
  • Caelia’s Breath: versatile exclamation for shock, frustration, or disbelief. Breath refers to pneuma as spiritually interpreted: the world’s or god’s breath.
  • Seas or Skies: replacement for “Heads or Tails,” based on the Imperial Glint coin.

Racket

Racket is an offshoot of Imperial Lycosian influenced by Fioran mercenaries and tradesmen. Unlike Imperial, it has roots in anarchistic culture, especially among people opposed to territorial conflicts between eastern and western Khorathian nations.

The name “Racket” began as a derogatory term before early speakers claimed it. It was formerly known as Mixed Lycosian because it hybridised Lycosian and Fioran.

Racket features

  • Adds a soft vowel sound after some words ending in consonants. For example, “air” may be pronounced roughly “air-eh.” This is more common among native Fioran speakers than Lycosian speakers.
  • Omits H sounds entirely. In writing, Racket often uses apostrophes in place of H: “has,” “hear,” and “hop” become “'as,” “'ear,” and “'op.”

Open questions
